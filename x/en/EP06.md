# Episode 6. IT Says Six Months

Tuesday, 9 a.m. A meeting room on the 17th floor, IT Platform Group.

Director Noh Seunghwan, Head of the IT Platform Group, did not open his laptop. Instead he set a single sheet of A4 paper in the middle of the table. One number was written on it by hand.

*6*

§IMG1§

"Six months."

Han Jiseok and Lee Woojin had not even finished sitting down.

"Director Noh, we haven't told you what we need yet."

"I know. I read Saturday's minutes. Nine clicks to checkout, down to three. Six months."

Noh was forty-five. Eighteen years at Cheongyeon. In that time he had torn down and rebuilt the brand mall three times, and all three times he had heard marketing shout "within two weeks." Those eighteen years sat under his eyes.

"And to be clear, six months is me being optimistic."

Woojin wrote *6 months (optimistic)* in his notebook. He thought about crossing it out, then left it.

"May I ask why?"

Han asked. His voice was calm. It was the voice he used at the research center every time an experiment failed.

Noh folded down his fingers one by one.

"One, the member system was built in 2012. To add guest checkout, we have to rewrite the payment module first. Two, identity verification is a security team rule. It came in after a personal data incident in 2015, so I can't just remove it. Three, the seven terms-and-conditions boxes are what Legal wants. Four, the pop-ups..."

He paused.

"The pop-ups come from every business division. Yeonhwi wants theirs, Slowash wants theirs. Every one of them says their pop-up has to be on the first screen. Do you know how many meetings I've held to sort that out?"

"How many?"

"Eleven. All eleven ended with 'to be re-discussed after consultation between divisions.'"

Woojin thought of the minutes, v1.0 through v7.0. Some meetings could run eleven times without a conclusion. Others could erase "Answer insufficient" in half a day.

Han said nothing for a while. Then he opened his laptop and turned the screen toward Noh. A table was on it. The nine steps he had clicked through himself at Saturday's meeting.

"Director, let's look at this together."

The table had three columns. Step, Content, and *Who made it*. The last column was empty.

"I'd like to fill in, with you, how many of the nine IT actually made."

Noh narrowed his eyes. Then, reluctantly, he leaned toward the screen.

Step 1, event pop-up. "Business divisions." Step 2, the skincare menu. "UX design. That one's ours." Step 3, the brand pages. "The divisions asked for separate brand pages." Step 4, product details. "That one obviously has to be there." Step 5, forced login. "Security and marketing. Because member count is a KPI." Step 6, sign-up. "Same reason." Step 7, identity verification. "Security." Step 8, the cart reset.

§IMG2§

Noh stopped.

"That... is a bug."

"A bug?"

"When verification finishes, a new session starts and the cart is wiped. We've known since last year. It kept getting pushed down the priority list."

"Who pushed it down?"

"The divisions said their pop-up requests were more urgent."

An odd silence settled over the room. Woojin held back a laugh. He knew it was not the moment to laugh, but with each step's reason coming to light, he felt he could see the screws of a huge machine called a company.

Step 9, the coupon confirmation window.

"Who put this one in?"

For the first time, Noh smiled.

"Your team, Senior Manager."

"Sorry?"

"The Online Strategy Team. Last year you wanted to raise coupon usage, so you asked us to always show a coupon window right before payment. The request has Director Baek Seungwon's approval on it."

Han couldn't say a word. Woojin wrote in his notebook: *Step 9 - us.*

The last column was full. Divisions, four. Security, one. Marketing, two. Our team, one. Bug, one. The only thing IT had made on its own was the menu structure.

"Director."

Han closed the laptop.

"The work that takes six months, let's take six months. I'll tell the President exactly that."

"Then what?"

"I'd like to pick out only what can be done in two weeks. From these nine."

Noh looked at Han for a long time. Then he leaned back in his chair.

"In eighteen years, marketing has told me 'get it done in two weeks' hundreds of times. You're the first one who's said 'let's choose together what can be done in two weeks.'"

That morning the two of them spent three hours in front of the table. Woojin brought coffee four times.

What could be done in two weeks.

The cart bug. "Three days. We should have fixed it long ago." The coupon window. "We just take that out," Han said. "I'll talk to Director Baek." The event pop-ups. "Technically, thirty minutes. Politically, six months," Noh said.

"For the pop-ups we only need the divisions to agree, right?"

"Only. But I've told you - we couldn't get that agreement in eleven meetings."

"This time we can say it's on the President's meeting agenda. That might change things."

Noh shrugged. It meant *go ahead and try*.

Forced login and sign-up would be replaced with quick guest checkout. Instead of rewriting the payment module, they could attach an outside easy-pay service. "The security review takes two weeks. But Director Mo Taejun in Information Security joined the company the same year I did," Noh said. For the first time, there was mischief in his voice.

11:50. A message from Kwon Jaehyuk arrived on Han's phone.

[Kwon Jaehyuk] How far along is the table?

[Han Jiseok] Sorting out what's possible in two weeks. I'll check the security review timeline and send the final version tomorrow morning.

[Kwon Jaehyuk] If it's 80% now, send it now.

[Kwon Jaehyuk] 90% tomorrow morning means nothing. The cooperation email to the divisions has to go out this afternoon.

[Kwon Jaehyuk] I'll fix whatever's wrong.

Han set his phone down and looked at Noh.

"Director, I'll assume the two-week security review is possible and send it now. If it's wrong, I'll take responsibility and fix it."

"Is that how you work?"

"Today is the first time I've worked that way."

Noh laughed. The table went to the VP's office right there, at 80%. Five minutes later, a reply came.

§IMG3§

[Kwon Jaehyuk] Got it. Good work.

Woojin glanced at the message and was surprised. He had never seen Kwon write "good work." Han wasn't surprised. He said he had heard those words about three times in twelve years.

Around lunchtime, a new line appeared under the table.

*Two-week goal: 9 steps → 5 steps.*

Not three. But not nine, either.

"The VP promised three," Woojin said carefully. Han answered while saving the table.

"The VP couldn't hear 'six months' in that room. That's why he said three. Five is the real number."

"Then what do we tell the President?"

"The real number. And honestly, that the last two are six-month work."

On the way back from lunch, Han stopped by Baek Seungwon's desk. Woojin followed.

"Director, do you remember asking for a coupon window right before payment last year?"

Baek took his eyes off his monitor and thought for a while.

"Ah, that. We had to raise coupon usage. That was the KPI back then."

"That's one of the nine steps."

"What?"

"Step nine. The very last one. Right before the pay button."

Baek's mouth slowly fell open. Then he leaned back and looked up at the ceiling.

"So... I'm the culprit."

"I wouldn't say culprit..."

"No, I'm the culprit. I signed it."

He burst out laughing. He laughed so loudly that people in the next part looked up. After a while he wiped his eyes and said,

"Take it out. Take it out now. If the President asks, give him my name."

"I won't give your name, Director."

"Why not?"

"I'll just say our team made it. Because that's true."

Baek looked at Han for a moment. Then he nodded. In a company, there were many ways to tell the truth. Woojin was learning that difference for the third time that week.

1:30 p.m. A message came from Noh.

[I had lunch with Director Mo Taejun from Information Security. He'll finish the easy-pay review within two weeks. But there's one condition.]

[What is it?]

[At next month's security training, he wants our team to give a talk. 'How Marketing and Security Can Get Along.' Nobody else will do it.]

Han showed the message to Woojin and laughed. This was how work at the company got done in the end. Not with an approval document, but with one lunch and one talk nobody wanted to give.

2 p.m. Woojin sent an email to six business divisions.

[Subject: [Follow-up to the President's Online Innovation Meeting] Request for cooperation - temporary removal of brand mall first-screen pop-ups]

He clicked send, leaned back, and let out a breath. He thought the first job was done.

Ten minutes later, the phone on Kwon Jaehyuk's desk rang.

§IMG4§

Even from Woojin's desk, he could hear the voice on the other end. That was how loud it was.

"VP Kwon! You want me to take down the Yeonhwi pop-up? And what is this? Yeonhwi is showing a 10% discount on the brand mall. Who did this?"

It was VP Eom Gitae.

The blood drained from Woojin's face. Yesterday afternoon, to test the idea of giving first-time buyers a coupon instead of a pop-up, he had registered one coupon. *Test only, Slowash only.* That was how he thought he had set it.

With shaking hands he opened the admin screen.

Coupon scope: All brands.

(End of Episode 6)
