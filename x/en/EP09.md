# Episode 9. The Video Call from Jakarta

Tuesday, 4:30 p.m. Thirty minutes before the meeting.

Na Seohyun came over to Lee Woojin's desk with her phone in her hand.

"Manager Lee, look at this before the meeting. How people in Indonesia buy cosmetics."

Short videos were playing one after another on the screen. A Jakarta influencer was putting on cream in front of her phone camera, live. Comments in Indonesian poured down the bottom of the screen, and a *Buy now* button blinked off to one side. Thousands of people were watching a single video at the same time.

"These are all SODA LAB and MINT&. People buy during the stream. Two taps to checkout."

"Two?"

"Yes. Ours was nine, remember? Five now."

§IMG1§

SODA LAB had caught up with Cheongyeon's Sugyeol in overseas sales within five years of being founded. MINT& and DAYBREEZE were close behind. All three had names that sounded light and fresh. They didn't put their weight on two Chinese characters the way Cheongyeon's brands did.

Woojin watched the video for a long time. Then he asked.

"Has anyone at headquarters ever used this app?"

Seohyun thought for a moment.

"I think you just became the first, sir."

Tuesday, 5 p.m. That was 3 p.m. in Jakarta and 4 p.m. in Singapore.

There were six people on the screen in the headquarters meeting room. Senior Manager Sung Dongmin from Corporate Planning, Manager Lim Soyeon and Assistant Manager Ryu Jaemin from the Overseas Biz. Team, and Han Jiseok and Lee Woojin from the Online Planning Part. Kwon Jaehyuk was in another meeting. The minutes, once again, were Woojin's job.

Sung Dongmin was thirty-nine. He had managed the numbers of the overseas subsidiaries in Corporate Planning for eight years. He talked fast, and his report sentences were clean. Lim Soyeon was a seventh-year manager in charge of Indonesia. She had lived in Jakarta for two years as an expat employee, so nobody at headquarters knew the local situation better. Ryu Jaemin was a third-year assistant manager who kept an Excel window open the whole meeting, typing in numbers.

In the top right of the screen was Daniel Tan from the Asia-Pacific regional office in Singapore. E-commerce director for the region. A forty-five-year-old Chinese Singaporean who looked at online sales for six Southeast Asian countries on a single screen.

And in the middle of the screen was Ayu Lestari from the Jakarta subsidiary. E-commerce manager at Cheongyeon Indonesia. Thirty-one. Her hijab was neatly wrapped, and behind her was an office wall that looked like part of a warehouse. On the wall, a Cheongyeon poster had come loose at one corner and curled.

§IMG2§

This meeting had started with a question President Moon Hyekyung threw out at Saturday's meeting. *Why does North America work and Southeast Asia doesn't?* The Overseas Biz. Team had decided to look for the answer in Indonesia. A population of about 270 million, the fourth-largest in the world. The largest Muslim-majority country on earth, where more than eight in ten people are Muslim. A halal product assurance law had been in force since 2019, and within a few years cosmetics would need halal certification too. A young country with an average age around thirty, where Korean dramas and K-beauty were just exploding. And Cheongyeon's sales weren't keeping up with any of that heat.

"Ayu, could you walk us through the marketplace first?"

Sung said it in English. His pronunciation was smooth.

Ayu shared her screen. Search results on Indonesia's biggest online marketplace. The search term was 'Korean moisturizing cream.'

Cheongyeon was not in the first row. Not in the second, either. SODA LAB, MINT&, and DAYBREEZE came up first. Two-thirds of Cheongyeon's price. 4.8 stars. Tens of thousands of reviews. And a *Tomorrow delivery* badge.

Cheongyeon's Sugyeol cream showed up only after four scrolls. 4.5 stars, a few hundred reviews. Delivery: '7-10 days.'

"Our products come from Korea by sea. SODA LAB keeps stock in a local warehouse here in Jakarta. So they deliver tomorrow."

Ayu's English was clear and deliberate. The English of someone who worked in the field. Nothing decorated.

"And this is our product page."

The screen changed. The Sugyeol cream product page. Woojin narrowed his eyes. Small English translations had been stuck on top of images made in Korean. No Indonesian anywhere.

"We use the images made in Seoul as they are. To translate them into Bahasa Indonesia, we need approval from headquarters. We asked four months ago."

"I'll look into that."

Sung said it. Then went straight on to his next sentence.

"But Manager Ayu, isn't the real issue visibility? If we're not on the first page of search, a good product page won't matter. Why don't you look at spending more on ads? Headquarters can adjust the budget for you."

Ryu Jaemin put it into English. Ayu was silent for a moment. On the screen, her eyes widened very slightly, then went back to normal.

"We already doubled the ad budget last quarter. Traffic went up, but people come in and leave. They leave when they see seven days of shipping. They leave when they see no Bahasa."

"Then how about changing the ad creative? Use local influencers, maybe."

"Influencers won't take our products."

"Why not?"

"Because we have no answer on halal certification. I sent the question to headquarters three weeks ago. No reply yet."

Lim Soyeon looked up. She added briefly.

"Indonesia has the largest Muslim population in the world. Word keeps going around there that cosmetics without halal certification will find it harder and harder to get into the big retail channels. It makes sense that influencers are careful."

Woojin wrote it down. Three weeks. Halal. No answer. Then he looked at Sung's face. It was the face of someone hearing this for the first time.

"I'll check with Quality on that. But in the short term, improving ad efficiency is still..."

That was when Daniel Tan in Singapore turned on his mic for the first time.

"From the regional view, Indonesia is the biggest opportunity we have in Southeast Asia. But my budget covers six countries. If Indonesia needs a local warehouse and halal, that is not an ad problem. That is a headquarters decision."

The room went quiet for a moment. Daniel had passed the ball straight back to Seoul. Sung touched his tie once.

"We'll review it and get back to you next week."

Sung brought up the ad budget two more times after that. Ayu brought up shipping, language, and halal two more times. They were looking at the same screen, but they seemed to work for two different companies.

Han Jiseok cut in for the first time.

"Ayu, how do SODA LAB and MINT& run their local warehouses? Do they do it themselves?"

"No. They outsource to local logistics companies. Some of those brands have only three or four people here. But they decide fast. In one day."

One day. Woojin underlined the words. He worked for a company that had taken three days to get its minutes from v1.0 to v7.0.

The meeting ended at 6:10. Sung wrapped it up.

"We'll summarize today's discussion around ways to improve ad efficiency and share it. Thank you, everyone."

The faces went off one by one. Sung, Daniel Tan, the Overseas Biz. Team, Han Jiseok. Woojin was about to press *End* when he stopped. Ayu was still on the screen. She was looking at him.

"Excuse me. Are you the one writing the minutes?"

"Yes. I'm Woojin Lee."

"Woojin."

Ayu said his name slowly.

"Please write what I said. Not what they wanted to hear."

§IMG3§

Woojin didn't know what to say. She gave a small smile. The smile of someone who was tired.

"Last time, the minutes said, 'The Indonesia team will improve ad efficiency.' I never said that. So nobody solved the shipping problem. Nobody answered the halal question. For six months."

"...I understand."

"Thank you, Woojin."

The screen went dark. Alone in the room, Woojin looked at his own face reflected in the black laptop screen.

8 p.m. He wrote the minutes.

'1. Local delivery takes 7-10 days (SODA LAB and MINT& use local warehouses in Jakarta for next-day delivery)
2. Request to translate product pages into Indonesian - waiting 4 months for headquarters approval
3. Halal certification question - no answer from headquarters for 3 weeks → no collaboration with local influencers possible
4. Ad budget doubled last quarter - traffic up, but visitors keep leaving
5. (Regional office) Local warehouse and halal certification are headquarters decisions
6. (Corporate Planning) Proposal to review ad efficiency'

Ad efficiency was line six. In the order Ayu had said things.

9 p.m. Director Baek Seungwon came to his desk. He read the minutes. For a long time he said nothing.

"Senior Manager Sung isn't going to like this."

"No, sir."

"Corporate Planning would've wanted this wrapped up as 'ad efficiency.' That way none of the headquarters departments get any work out of it."

"I know."

Baek read it once more. And he didn't say *take this out*, the way he had last week.

"Just send it."

"Sir?"

"This time, just send it. The person on the ground asked you herself."

Woojin pressed send. Eleven people on the distribution list.

11 p.m. On the subway home, his phone buzzed. Yoon Chaewon.

[Night shift today. Did you eat?]

[Yeah. You?]

[Who has time to eat in the ER. But I think you sound good today.]

[How do you know?]

[There's a period at the end of your message. You don't use periods when you're in a bad mood.]

Woojin laughed at his reflection in the subway window. Seven years together, and she could read him from a single period.

At the same moment, on a different subway, Choi Yoona was gripping her phone. A message from her daughter's daycare teacher, Ms. Song Yujin.

[Mom, Seoa has had a slight fever since the afternoon. 38°C. We gave her fever medicine, but please check again tomorrow morning.]

Tomorrow was Wednesday. Two days left until Friday. Yoona hoped the fever would come down within those two days.

It didn't.

(End of Episode 9)
