# Episode 11. This Is Just the Beginning

Moon Hyekyung wrote something at the top of page 9 in red pen.

Woojin couldn't see it from where he stood. She put the pen down and spoke.

"When a child is sick, you stay home."

A few people in the room looked up.

"Next time, tell us on Friday. And decide who presents in your place. Like today."

Nobody in the room was sure whether that was kindness or a warning. But Woojin knew one thing. At that moment, Choi Yoona's name had come out of the President's mouth. And that name would now be remembered together with a sick child. Whether that was good or bad, he couldn't tell.

"Go on."

Woojin reported the rest of the numbers. Checkout drop-off. In the three days since the steps went down to five, noticeably fewer customers reached the payment screen and then left. Guest checkout passed a quarter of all orders. After the pop-ups for the five brands other than Yeonhwi came down, fewer people left straight from the first screen.

"Three days is short. Next week I'll report again with a full week of data."

Just as Yoona's notes said. Admit first that three days is short.

Hyekyung closed the report. Then she looked around the room. Kwon Jaehyuk, Baek Seungwon, Han Jiseok, and Woojin, still standing. Last, the empty side seat by the door. The seat Yoona had taken last week.

"This is just the beginning."

That was all. She stood up and left the room.

7:41 a.m. Seven minutes earlier than last week.

For three seconds after the door closed, nobody moved. Then Baek Seungwon leaned back in his chair and let out a long breath. It was so loud that VP Oh Jeongtaek, the head of Finance sitting across from him, laughed. The laughter spread. People from Corporate Planning, IT and the Overseas Biz. Team got up one by one and nodded at Kwon Jaehyuk on their way out. The first time in three weeks.

Only VP Eom Gitae left without any expression. At the door he put his printout down on the table and walked away. On the line that read "five brands other than Yeonhwi," the pen had dug in deep.

CEO Hwang Inseok came over to Kwon Jaehyuk.

"VP Kwon, good work. I'll present these numbers myself at next week's CEO meeting."

"Yes, sir."

"And Manager Lee."

Hwang looked at Woojin.

"You got stuck on the first sentence, but it was fine. Next time, don't get stuck."

He left smiling. The smile was exactly half praise and half warning. For the first time, Woojin could read the ratio.

At the elevators, someone tugged lightly at Woojin's sleeve. Manager Lim Soyeon from the Overseas Biz. Team. She had handled Indonesia for seven years. She had barely said a word during Tuesday's video call. Today she spoke first.

"Manager Lee, thank you for Tuesday's minutes."

"Sorry?"

"Ayu emailed yesterday. She said for the first time she sent headquarters minutes around to her own team. Because her words were written down exactly as she said them."

Soyeon took out her phone and showed him one line from the email. A single sentence in English.

'For the first time, Seoul heard us.'

"Seven years, and one page of minutes did what I couldn't."

She smiled, but the end of the smile was a little bitter. Assistant Manager Ryu Jaemin, standing behind her, gave a quick bow. Third year at the company, the youngest on the Overseas Biz. Team. He pointed at page 9 of Woojin's printout and spoke quietly.

"Sir, this five-step checkout. Could we put it on the Jakarta mall as it is?"

Woojin couldn't answer. The elevator doors opened. The question stayed with him long after they closed.

8:10 a.m. A hangover-soup place behind the office, 「Seven O'Clock Haejangguk」.

'No groups of five or more.' The four of them sat at one table. Kwon Jaehyuk ordered without looking at the menu.

"Four haejangguk. One plate of suyuk. And..."

He hesitated for a moment.

"One bottle of soju."

"Sir, it's eight in the morning."

That was Baek Seungwon. His face was smiling, whatever his words said.

"For us it's Friday night. Three weeks running."

The soju came. Kwon poured all four glasses himself. It was the first time a VP had poured Woojin a drink. He took it with both hands. His hands were still shaking a little.

The soup came in black earthenware bowls, still bubbling, red with chili and thick with cabbage and congealed ox blood. Steam rose between them and fogged Woojin's glasses. The suyuk, thin slices of boiled pork, came stacked on a wooden board with salted shrimp on the side. Nobody had eaten since before five.

"Manager Lee."

"Yes, sir."

"That first sentence earlier."

Woojin's face went red.

"I'm sorry. In the minutes..."

"In the minutes, 'Manager Lee Woojin reported results of checkout improvements.' One line is enough."

Kwon raised his glass.

"The part where you got stuck, only the four of us need to know."

Han Jiseok laughed first. Baek Seungwon laughed too. Woojin laughed, and then suddenly the backs of his eyes went hot and he lowered his head. Nobody pointed it out.

The four of them clinked glasses. This time there was no one-meter gap between them.

When they were halfway through the soup, Kwon's phone buzzed. A message. He looked at the screen and smiled for a moment. A real smile. Woojin had never seen it before.

"It's the President."

The other three stopped their spoons.

"'Check that Manager Choi's child is all right, and let me know on Monday.'"

The restaurant went quiet. Baek Seungwon took out his phone first.

"I'll call Yoona right now."

"No, I'll do it."

Kwon went outside the restaurant to make the call. Through the glass door they could see him bow his head a few times. Woojin couldn't tell if it was an apology or a greeting. Probably both.

Kwon came back.

"Seoa's fever is down. She's asleep now."

The four of them split what was left of the soju. Then Kwon set his phone on the table.

"And the next assignment."

"Already?"

"There was one more line in the President's message."

He read it straight off the screen.

"'Next month, why Southeast Asia and Latin America aren't working. In the voices of the people on the ground.'"

Woojin thought of Tuesday's video call. Ayu Lestari's face. The eyes under the hijab that didn't choose their words carefully. 'Please write what I said.' Those minutes had gone out to eleven places. One of them was the President's office.

Han Jiseok looked at Woojin.

"She read your minutes."

Instead of answering, Woojin emptied his glass. The soju tasted strangely sweet.

On his way back to the office, Kwon took out his phone. It was 2 a.m. in London. His daughter would probably be asleep. He wrote a message.

[Hayoung, it's Dad. I don't think I can come over this Easter break either. Something came up at work. I'm sorry.]

Before he pressed send, he stared at the last sentence for a long time. He had sent the same sentence last summer break, and last winter break. After "I'm sorry" he wrote one more line and deleted it. 'Next time for sure.' Words he wasn't sure he could keep.

He sent the message. In the London dawn, the little "1" beside it, the sign that it hadn't been read, stayed where it was.

10 a.m. The four of them parted in front of the restaurant. Han Jiseok to his son's swimming class, Baek Seungwon home, Kwon back to the office. Woojin started walking toward the subway station and took out his phone.

[Chaewon, are you free tonight?]

[I'm off today. Why?]

[I have something to tell you. What I tried to say last time and didn't.]

The reply took a minute.

[Finally? 7 o'clock. If you're late, it's really over.]

He smiled and turned around. Not toward the subway station. Toward the office. The 29th floor, the bottom drawer of his desk. This time he wouldn't forget.

On Monday morning, a stranger was standing in front of Woojin's desk.

Director Cho Youngseok from the Daily Beauty Biz. Division. The man who had talked about apartments at the window table on the night of the split dinner. He peered at Woojin's monitor and smiled.

"Manager Lee, I hear you put together an online-only proposal last week? A bulk body wash subscription. Could you send me that file?"

(End of Episode 11)
