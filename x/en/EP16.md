# Episode 16. Things Written Alone

Thursday, 2:14 a.m. In the upper left corner of Han Jiseok's laptop screen, a file name.

'Saturday Report_Emerging Markets Status_v12.pptx'

The twelfth version.

President Moon had asked one question last Saturday. North America and Europe are doing well, so why not Southeast Asia and Latin America? The Overseas Biz. Team had sent six slides, and four of them were about exchange rates and the situation with distributors. Jiseok had stretched those six slides to thirty, cut them back to twelve, and now they were at eighteen.

Versions 1 through 11 were piled up in a folder on his desktop. Each version had a different conclusion.

v3 - 'Weakness in emerging markets is due to structural factors.'
v7 - 'Lack of understanding of local distribution is the core cause.'
v11 - 'One-sided marketing guidelines from headquarters undermine local execution.'

After writing v11, he glared at the screen for a long time. That sentence was aimed straight at the Overseas Biz. Team. It was true. But not everything true could be written down.

A chair creaked on the other side of the partition. Jiseok looked up. It was Woojin.

"You're still here?"

"You're still here, sir."

"Go home. You have to set up the meeting tomorrow morning."

"Tomorrow's Thursday, sir."

Jiseok looked at the clock. He was right. It was Thursday. Two days left until Saturday. He pressed his palms against his eyes.

Woojin came around the partition to his desk. He looked at v12 on the screen.

"Sir, what number is that?"

"Twelve."

"You haven't shown us any of them."

Jiseok didn't answer. Woojin hesitated, then went on.

"Sir. We can write too."

"I know."

"If you know, why do you write it alone?"

The question was quiet but exact. Jiseok took his hands off the keyboard. Why did he write it alone? He knew the answer. He had just never said it out loud.

"If it's wrong, better that I'm the one who gets yelled at."

"Then when do we ever get yelled at?"

Jiseok looked at Woojin. Thirty-six. Nine years at the company. A junior who wrote perfect minutes but had never once written the conclusion of a report. Jiseok knew whose fault that was.

"...Go home. We'll talk in the morning."

Woojin bowed his head and went back. Jiseok looked at the screen again. The conclusion box was empty.

Thursday, 9 a.m. The VP's office.

Kwon Jaehyuk didn't turn the pages of the printout. He looked at the cover and set it down.

"It says v12."

"The file name printed as is. I'm sorry."

"Nothing to be sorry about. In twelve rewrites, who did you show it to?"

"...No one."

Kwon turned his chair to the window.

"Han. When I was in Hong Kong, my boss was British. Do you know what the first assignment he gave me was?"

"No, sir."

"Split one report among three juniors and don't touch it myself. Even if the result was seventy percent of what I'd write, send it up as it was."

"Why?"

"Because while I write a hundred percent, the three juniors learn zero. According to him, that was why I wasn't getting promoted."

Jiseok couldn't say anything.

"Split this report three ways. Numbers to Choi Yoona, field cases to Lee Woojin, competitors to Na Seohyun. You write only the one-line conclusion. And don't fix theirs. If it's at eighty percent, send it up."

"You want me to put eighty percent in front of the President?"

"The President doesn't want a hundred percent. She wants a team that can bring a hundred percent again next week. You can't do that alone. You'll collapse."

Kwon turned his chair back.

"And that v11. The sentence blaming the Overseas Biz. Team. Take it out. Even if it's true."

"You saw it?"

"You uploaded it to the shared folder at three in the morning. I wasn't asleep either."

Thursday, 2 p.m. The part's meeting room.

Jiseok stood at the whiteboard. Choi Yoona, Woojin and Na Seohyun were sitting there. None of them had opened their laptops. They were looking at him. It was the first meeting like this.

"The emerging markets report for Saturday. The three of you split it."

Seohyun blinked. Yoona's face showed nothing. Only Woojin smiled, very slightly.

"Yoona, the numbers. Indonesia and Mexico. Sales, traffic, churn, average order value. Compared with competitors."

"Okay."

"Woojin, the field. Emails from the overseas subsidiaries, customer inquiries, reviews. Not numbers. People's voices."

"Okay."

"Seohyun, how SODA LAB and MINT& sell in those countries. Live commerce, influencers, prices."

"I'm writing too?"

"You're writing. Your slides go up under your name."

Seohyun closed her mouth. Then she wrote something in big letters in her notebook.

"Send me yours by 5 p.m. tomorrow. I'll write only the one-line conclusion. And..."

Jiseok took a breath.

"I won't fix them. If it's at eighty percent, it goes up."

Yoona looked up for the first time.

"Sir, really?"

"Really."

"Then can I put in all my numbers? You won't tell me to pick only three like last time?"

Jiseok laughed. He always cut Yoona's numbers down to three. The President doesn't like a lot of numbers, he'd say. Maybe the one who didn't like numbers had been him.

"Up to five."

"Seven."

"Six."

"...Deal."

Friday, 5:02 p.m. Three files arrived in Jiseok's inbox.

Yoona's slide had six numbers. Exactly six. Woojin's slide quoted one line from an email by Carlos Mendoza at the Mexico subsidiary, in large type. Seohyun's slide had a different design. She hadn't used the part's template.

Jiseok's fingers itched. He wanted to fix the font sizes on Seohyun's slide. He wanted to change how Woojin cited his quote. He wanted to change the colors of Yoona's graph.

He took his hand off the mouse. And on the empty first slide he wrote only the one-line conclusion.

'The problem in emerging markets is not price. It's that we don't know how our customers buy.'

The file name was v1.

Saturday, 8 a.m. The boardroom.

President Moon was turning the slides when she stopped at Seohyun's.

"Only this slide has a different design."

The air in the room stiffened slightly. Just as Jiseok was about to speak, President Moon spoke first.

"This one is the easiest to read. Use this format from now on."

Seohyun's face turned red.

After the meeting, Kwon stopped Jiseok in front of the elevators.

"Han. What percent was that report?"

"By my standard, seventy."

"By the President's?"

Jiseok couldn't answer. Kwon stepped into the elevator and said,

"I don't know either. But today the President picked up her red pen only three times. Last week it was eleven."

The doors closed. Jiseok stood there for a long time.

That night he didn't delete the folder on his desktop that held v1 through v12. Instead, he renamed it.

'Things I wrote alone.'

(End of Episode 16)
