# Episode 4. One Line in the Minutes

§IMG1§

9:30 on Saturday morning. Everyone else had gone home. Only two of them were left in the VP's office.

Kwon Jaehyuk pulled off his tie and tossed it onto the desk. It was a gesture no one at the company ever saw.

"Seungwon, sit down."

Only then did Baek Seungwon loosen his own tie. When they were alone, Kwon spoke to him casually, and Baek called him *hyung* - big brother. It had been that way since they first met fifteen years ago on a business trip to Hong Kong and drank through the night. Inside the company, only one person knew: Han Jiseok.

"Hyung, that silence today went on a little long."

"I know."

"Jiseok was about to speak. You stopped him."

"If he opens his mouth, ten reasons come out. Every one of them true. That's why I stopped him. In that room, a true reason is the most dangerous thing there is."

Kwon looked out the window. Below them lay the Gwanghwamun intersection. On a Saturday morning, the square was empty.

"Seungwon. The three clicks I promised - I can't keep that."

"I know."

"Five would be a good result. But in front of the President, I had to say three."

"Then next week, tell her you were wrong."

Kwon turned to look at him.

"If you say you were wrong, hyung, I think the President will actually like it. Nobody in that room ever says they were wrong."

Kwon stared at Baek for a long moment, then laughed. It was a laugh no one had ever seen in the conference room.

"That's why you're still a Director."

"And that's why you're a VP."

They both laughed. Then Kwon added,

"But that youngest one. Manager Lee."

"Woojin?"

"Did you see his hands shaking? When he lent his phone to VP Eom."

"I saw."

"He was still the first to hand it over."

Kwon said only that, then picked up his tie again. Baek didn't ask whether it was praise. From this man, praise usually came in about that length.

11 p.m. on Sunday. A message came from Yoon Chaewon.

§IMG2§

[What were you about to say yesterday?]

Woojin lay in bed and looked at that sentence for a long time. At Mayday Table, he had slipped his hand into his inside jacket pocket and pulled it out again. She hadn't missed it. Those were the eyes of a nurse who had lasted seven years in a university hospital ER.

[It's nothing. I'll tell you next time.]

[When is next time?]

He couldn't write an answer. There was a meeting next Saturday. And the Saturday after that. The name of the group chat came to mind. *Saturday, 7 A.M. (1/52).*

[Sorry. Before the end of this month, for sure.]

The 1 disappeared. A long while later, a reply came.

[You say sorry way too often these days.]

6:50 on Monday morning. Woojin was the first to arrive on the 29th floor. As soon as he sat down, he opened the bottom drawer of his desk. The navy box was still there. He opened it, checked the ring, closed it, and pushed the drawer shut. A man who kept a ring at the office. That was who he was now.

§IMG3§

When he turned on his laptop, Saturday's draft minutes came up.

The file name was *(Minutes) 1st Online Innovation Meeting (210320)_v1.0*. Before opening it, he made a copy. If ten years at a company had taught him anything, it was that minutes are never finished in one pass.

The cursor had stopped on one line.

*Open item: cause of the nine-step checkout - answer insufficient.*

"Don't write that."

A voice behind him. Director Baek. He was holding two cups of coffee and looking down at Woojin's monitor. There was no telling how long he'd been standing there.

"Sir, you're in early."

"If I come in later than you, the VP chews me out. Says I'm making the juniors come in early."

Baek set one coffee down, pulled over a chair, and sat.

"'Answer insufficient.' Delete it."

"But it's true. The President asked why, and nobody answered."

"Woojin. Do you know who reads these minutes?"

Woojin pictured the distribution list. The President's office, the CEO's office, Corporate Planning, Finance, the Yeonhwi Division, the Daily Beauty Division, the IT Platform Group. Eleven in all.

"Eleven places."

"And those eleven places will each read those two words differently. The President's office reads, *homework left over.* The CEO's office reads, *they weren't ready.* Yeonhwi reads, *that guy is wobbling.* VP Eom will run a highlighter over that line."

"But the record should be accurate..."

"A record isn't there to keep the facts."

Baek took a sip of coffee.

"It's there to keep the next meeting."

Woojin had no answer. What Baek said seemed wrong, and yet it was right. And it seemed right, and yet something about it was uncomfortable.

9 a.m. The first revision request came in. It was Cha Mijeong, secretary in the CEO's office of Cheongyeon Cosmetics.

"Manager Lee, I read the draft minutes. The CEO just wants one word changed. Where you wrote that the President *reprimanded* us."

"Yes."

"Please change it to *encouraged*."

"When the President said, 'How many leave somewhere in those six?' - that was encouragement?"

A short laugh on the other end of the line.

"In the CEO's words, it was encouragement. And please take out the line *CEO: identity verification in progress.*"

Woojin deleted the line. The scene of the CEO waiting for a verification text on his own company's online store vanished from the company's history.

10:30 a.m. The second revision request. The Yeonhwi Division. VP Eom Gitae's secretary sent an email.

[In the minutes, the President's remark that "the daughters of Yeonhwi customers don't go to department stores" omits its context and may be misunderstood. Please summarize it as "remarks on the need to broaden the customer base."]

2 p.m. The third. Corporate Planning.

[The figures "three" and "nine" lack a clear basis for comparison. We request softer wording: "room for improvement identified."]

Lunch. In the company cafeteria, Na Seohyun set her tray down across from Woojin.

"Manager Lee, what version are the minutes on now?"

"Fifth."

"Wow."

Seohyun looked genuinely impressed. Back when she was a beauty YouTuber, she said, she had never revised an edit more than twice before uploading a video.

"A friend from my training cohort works at SODA LAB. She says they don't have minutes. Everything stays in the messenger channel. Who said what, exactly as they said it."

"So what if someone asks for a change?"

"You can't change it. If you edit a message, it shows *edited*."

Woojin's spoon stopped. He couldn't quite picture a company like that.

"But then wouldn't everyone be careful about what they say?"

"Yes. So their meetings are short. Fifteen minutes."

Choi Yoona, listening from the next seat, laughed.

"Seohyun, if our company did that, meetings would be zero minutes. Nobody would say anything."

The three of them laughed. Even as he laughed, Woojin thought: a company where people are careful before they speak, and a company where people fix things after they speak. Which one was more honest?

4 p.m. The file name had become *(Minutes) 1st Online Innovation Meeting (210320)_v6.0*. Every time he changed a word, the number went up by one.

Woojin opened v1.0 and v6.0 side by side. v1.0 had *three*, *nine*, *reprimanded*, *the daughters of Yeonhwi customers*, *answer insufficient*. v6.0 had *room for improvement*, *encouraged*, *broaden the customer base*, and not a single open item.

§IMG4§

The two documents recorded the same meeting. And yet they didn't seem like the same meeting.

"So you're learning the company language."

Han Jiseok glanced at the monitor from the next desk.

"Sir, is this right?"

"There's no right or wrong. Everyone does it."

"But then later, if someone reads these minutes, they won't know what happened that day."

Han stopped for a moment.

"That's why a company keeps two sets of minutes."

"Two?"

"One in the shared folder. The other..."

He tapped his temple twice with a finger.

"In here."

5 p.m. Woojin sent the final version to Kwon. Five minutes later, Kwon called him into his office.

The office was smaller than he'd expected. Instead of documents, the desk held cosmetics samples. Five Cheongyeon products, five from SODA LAB and MINT&. Every lid was open.

"Manager Lee, I read the minutes."

"Thank you, sir."

"The open items are gone."

Sweat beaded on Woojin's back.

"Director Baek... no, I decided on my own..."

"Director Baek told you to take them out. He always does that. He clears away anything that might hurt someone before they get there."

Kwon picked up a moisturizing cream and rubbed it on the back of his hand. A SODA LAB product.

"But Manager Lee. The President remembers that we couldn't answer her *why*. If it's not in the minutes, she'll also remember that we erased it."

"Then... should I put it back in?"

"No."

Kwon rubbed the cream into his skin. Then he put Cheongyeon's Sugyeol cream on the other hand.

"You decide. The minutes go out under your name."

Woojin left the office and sat back down at his desk. The cursor blinked.

At the bottom of v6.0, he added one line back.

*Open item: cause of the nine-step checkout - root-cause analysis to be included in next week's report.*

*Insufficient* had become *to be included*. Words that were true and not true at the same time. A question no one had answered had become next week's promise. Only after changing those words did he realize he had just learned the company language for the first time.

He renamed the file. *(Minutes) 1st Online Innovation Meeting (210320)_v7.0*. The final version.

7 p.m. On his way out, Woojin saw that the door to the VP's office was half open. Han was standing inside. Kwon's voice carried out.

"Senior Manager Han, are you free for dinner tonight?"

"Yes, sir. I'm free."

"I wonder if that place is still there. The *sundae-guk* place behind the office - the pork soup place. Doldam House."

Han was quiet for a moment. Then he smiled. It was a smile Woojin had never seen before, the smile of someone meeting a person from a very long time ago.

"It's still there. Different owner, though."

"Then let's go there. It's been twelve years."

(End of Episode 4)
