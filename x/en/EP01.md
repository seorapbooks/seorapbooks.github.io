# Episode 1. Split Tables

§IMG1§

March 2021. Friday, 7 p.m.

On the second floor of Hwarodam, a charcoal-grill restaurant in Seongsu-dong, every table seated exactly four people.

The company was in Gwanghwamun, in Cheongyeon Tower. But team dinners were always in Seongsu. The younger staff said that eating barbecue in Gwanghwamun felt like overtime. Baek Seungwon had heard that and moved the venue. He was the kind of man who listened to things like that.

"You there - fifth person! Next table, please. No, not you, sir. The one beside you."

Lee Woojin stood at the door with a list, splitting people up. The Online Strategy Group had thirty-one people. Divided by four, that made seven tables with three left over. Where to put the three, he had revised the spreadsheet eleven times over the past three days.

Each table was booked under a different name. Each would get its own receipt. No private gatherings of five or more. Nobody knew for certain whether a team dinner counted as private or as work, but the company's answer was simple:

*Sit in groups of four or fewer. No moving between tables.*

"Manager Lee, this is a study hall."

It was Director Cho Youngseok, in charge of e-commerce for Slowash, the hair-and-body brand in the Daily Beauty division. He'd joined as a "guest" because his team shared the floor. He sat down, looked around, and clicked his tongue.

"There are five of us at home. So I can't even eat dinner in my own house? Well, our complex shut down its residents' dining hall too. That's the problem with living somewhere nice."

Nobody had asked about his apartment complex. Woojin smiled and guided him to the window table. It had the best ventilation, and, more importantly, it was farthest from everyone else.

7:10. Director Baek Seungwon, Head of the Online Strategy Biz. Group, stood up.

"All right, glasses up. Stay at your tables."

Thirty-one glasses rose at once from seven islands. To toast, you had to reach toward the next table, but with a meter between them the glasses never met. Someone clinked against thin air, and laughter spread.

"You all worked hard last year. This year, let's show them with numbers. But tonight - no talk about numbers. Cheers!"

"Cheers!"

That was Baek Seungwon. A man who saw people first. Ten years as a department-store buyer, ten more at Cheongyeon. However tangled the problem, he could loosen it with one bottle of soju at a dinner like this. This year, the people he could share that bottle with were capped at four.

At the next table, Associate Na Seohyun, the new hire, was filming the grill on her phone. Twenty-six, a former beauty YouTuber who had joined last fall. Rumor said she'd had thirty thousand subscribers. She always corrected it: "Twenty-nine thousand."

"Seohyun, don't post dinner photos. If it looks like more than five people, we're in trouble."

That was Manager Choi Yoona, who handled data. She didn't look up from her phone as she said it. On the screen, her six-year-old daughter was brushing her teeth in pajamas.

"Mom, when are you coming home?"

"Three bites of meat and I'm leaving. Go to sleep first."

"You said three bites last time."

Yoona ended the video call and ate exactly three pieces of meat. Then she picked up her bag. Seohyun watched and murmured,

"At SODA LAB they do team dinners on Zoom. Everyone orders delivery at home."

SODA LAB: the indie brand people in their twenties and thirties were buying most these days. A college friend of Seohyun's was a marketer there.

"Then who grills the meat?"

"Everyone grills their own."

Yoona laughed as she stood.

"Seohyun, that's exactly why this company isn't working. We're still deciding who grills the meat."

Woojin's table was the farthest one in. The other three seats were the problem.

VP Kwon Jaehyuk. Senior Manager Han Jiseok. And one empty chair.

Kwon, Head of the Online Strategy Biz. Team, hadn't arrived yet. No one wanted the seat beside him, so it went to Woojin, the most junior person and the one who had made the seating chart. That was the fate of a man who revised a spreadsheet eleven times.

"Woojin, you know how this works, right?"

Han Jiseok, across from him, turned the meat with tongs. Head of the Online Planning Part. He'd come to marketing from the research lab, where he used to formulate creams. He spoke calmly, and he grilled calmly.

"Know what?"

"Sit at the VP's table, and you're the one grilling."

"But you're the one grilling, sir."

"Exactly. I'm letting you practice."

Woojin laughed and took the tongs. His phone buzzed. Yoon Chaewon.

[Brunch tomorrow at 11. Our seventh anniversary. If you're late, I'm really going to be mad.]

Seven years. He had been with her, a nurse at a university hospital, for seven years. Every time marriage came up, work got there first. This time he wouldn't put it off. He had even bought a ring. It was in the bottom drawer of his desk at the office.

[Of course. Tomorrow I'm all yours.]

7:40. Kwon Jaehyuk walked in.

Black coat, mask. As he climbed the stairs, the seven islands went quiet one by one. Fifteen years at the Asia headquarters of a global beauty company, recruited to Cheongyeon two years ago. Quick to decide, short in his explanations, shorter still in his praise.

"Sit, sit. Don't get up for me."

He sat beside Woojin and accepted a glass. He took a sip, ate one piece of meat, and said briefly to Woojin,

"Nicely done."

Woojin turned the words over for about three seconds. It was the first praise he'd ever heard from this man.

Then Kwon's phone rang.

It was the company messenger tone. He looked down at the screen. His expression didn't change. Only the hand setting down his glass slowed, very slightly.

Woojin hadn't meant to look. But he was sitting right there.

[SVP & CEO Hwang Inseok] VP Kwon, President Moon will receive the online business report in person, starting tomorrow at 7 a.m. Main conference room, 29th floor. Please prepare the materials on your end. She says it will be every week from now on.

Tomorrow, 7 a.m. It was a Saturday.

President Moon Hyekyung. Chief of Strategy and Finance at the Cheongyeon Group holding company. Relentless over a single number, unsparing in her criticism. And she had said she would review the online business every week.

Kwon placed his phone face down and picked up another piece of meat. He chewed slowly. Only after he had swallowed did he look up.

§IMG2§

"Could you get Director Baek for me?"

Baek was at the window table, listening to Cho talk about his mortgage. When Woojin whispered in his ear, the smile left Baek's face very slowly.

No moving between tables. But Baek broke the rule for the first time. Becoming the fifth person, he crouched beside Kwon.

"The President? Tomorrow?"

"Tomorrow. And every week after."

"And the materials..."

"There aren't any. So we make them now."

Kwon looked at Han Jiseok. Jiseok had already set down the tongs.

"Han, can you pull a year of numbers for our online store right now?"

"Two hours, once I'm at the office."

"One hour."

"...An hour and a half."

The corner of Kwon's mouth lifted, just slightly. Woojin couldn't tell whether it was a smile.

"Let the dinner go on. Let everyone finish their drinks, and put the bill on Director Baek's card. Just the four of us go back."

Four. Woojin realized, a beat late, that he was included in that number.

"Me too?"

"You grill well."

Whether it was a joke, he couldn't tell this time either.

8:05. The four of them went quietly down the stairs. Laughter poured from the second floor. Someone shouted "Cheers!" and seven islands clinked glasses against the air. With that sound at their backs, the four of them hailed taxis.

§IMG3§

They split into two cabs. Four people would have fit in one, but Kwon insisted on riding alone.

"I need to think."

In the second cab, Baek looked out the window and murmured,

"If President Moon is reviewing it herself..."

He didn't finish. Han didn't ask. Only Woojin didn't know what waited at the end of that sentence.

Han took out his phone and called his wife. It rang three times, and then came the voice of Lim Soojin, an elementary-school teacher. Behind her, their nine-year-old son Doyoon was shouting something.

"Done already? That's a first."

"No, it's just starting. I'm going back to the office."

"...Now?"

"Yeah. And tomorrow morning too."

The line went quiet for a moment.

"Tomorrow is Doyoon's first swimming lesson."

"I know. I'm sorry."

"Tell Doyoon you're sorry yourself. I'll put him on."

Han told his son, *Daddy will definitely come next week.* Woojin pretended to look out the window and listened. And he wondered: would he really be able to go next week?

Baek turned around in the front seat.

"Jiseok, what time is Doyoon's lesson?"

"Nine."

"Then you can run over after the meeting. The President's report should be done in an hour."

How optimistic that was, none of the three knew at the time.

8:40 p.m. Cheongyeon Tower, 29th floor, Gwanghwamun.

Only their footsteps echoed in the dark office. Kwon stood at the whiteboard and uncapped a marker.

"Let me say one thing first."

He looked at each of the three in turn.

"Tomorrow's meeting isn't a report. It's a test. The President doesn't care what we've done. She's coming to see what we don't know."

"Then what should we prepare?"

Baek asked. Kwon touched the marker to the whiteboard.

"Write down ten questions the President will ask tomorrow. Starting with the ones we can't answer."

Han took the marker. He hesitated, then wrote the first line.

*Why have we ended up like this?*

No one laughed.

The questions grew one by one. Online sales went up - so why did market share fall? Why does North America work and Southeast Asia doesn't? Why does every brand have a different front page on the online store? Each time Baek wrote one, Kwon asked briefly, "Can you answer that with numbers right now?" There wasn't a single question they could answer.

Woojin went back to his desk and opened his laptop. His eyes went to the bottom drawer. Inside, at the back, was a small navy box. The ring he'd finally made up his mind to buy on payday last month. He meant to take it out at brunch tomorrow.

He opened the drawer a little, then closed it. Once tomorrow morning's meeting was over, he'd grab this, go home, shower, and head out. There was enough time. There would be.

Woojin took out his phone, wrote a message to Chaewon, and deleted it. He wrote it again.

§IMG4§

[Brunch tomorrow - I might be a little late. Really, just a little.]

The small "1" beside the message disappeared. No reply came.

That night, Woojin didn't know.

That the 7 a.m. Saturday meetings would go on for the next three years.

And that of the four people standing before this whiteboard tonight, two would leave the company three years later.

(End of Episode 1)
