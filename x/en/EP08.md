# Episode 8. The Gray Area

Choi Yoona's page had two graphs on it.

The one on the left was a line going up. Cheongyeon's online sales. They had climbed steadily through a year of COVID. The one on the right was a line going down. Department stores, duty-free, offline sales. It was falling much faster.

Between the two lines was an area shaded gray. What had gone down was bigger than what had gone up. The difference was the gray.

Under the graphs was a single line.

'Online is not growing the company. It is only slowing down how fast the company shrinks.'

Lee Woojin read the sentence three times. Coming in at 5:30 every Saturday morning. Cutting the checkout down one step at a time. All of it, the sentence said, only made the gray area a little narrower.

"Manager Choi, if we show this to the President..."

"What do you think she'll say?"

Yoona said it calmly over her coffee. Thirty-eight. Mother of a six-year-old daughter. The most accurate person in the company with numbers, and always the first to put the brakes on a meeting run on gut feeling.

"They say the President loves numbers. These are real numbers. And the twenty-two hours of the Yeonhwi coupon show a way to shrink the gray. Put them side by side and it becomes a story."

Friday, 10 p.m. The week-two report was finished.

Cover, summary, the nine-step analysis, a two-week action plan, a six-month task list. Then Yoona's graph on page 7 and the Yeonhwi twenty-two hours on page 8. This time Han Jiseok rewrote the conclusion only once. If anything had improved since last week, it was that.

Yoona left first, at 10:30. If her husband was going to take their daughter to kindergarten in the morning, she said, she had to pack the lunchbox tonight.

"I'll do the printing. See you at 5:30 tomorrow."

Woojin said it. Yoona said thank you, and then said sorry. People in this company said sorry far too often these days.

Saturday, 5:20 a.m. Woojin opened the final version in the shared folder. One last check before printing sixteen copies.

Page 1, page 2, and after page 6 came page 7. But page 7 was the Yeonhwi twenty-two hours.

He scrolled back up. Then down again. Yoona's gray graph was not there.

He opened the version history. Last edited: 11:40 last night. Edited by: Kwon Jaehyuk.

Woojin's finger stopped over the print button.

He called Han Jiseok. Two rings, then a voice not quite awake. Jiseok had already left home and was in a taxi.

"Sir, Manager Choi's page is gone. The VP deleted it last night."

The other end went quiet. Only the taxi radio, faintly.

"...If the VP took it out, he had a reason."

"Shouldn't we tell Manager Choi?"

"It's 5:30 in the morning. Yoona is asleep next to Seoa. You're going to wake her up and say what? 'Your page is gone, but I don't know why'?"

Woojin didn't answer.

"Print it, Woojin. We'll hear the reason from the VP ourselves after the meeting. That's the order of things."

The order of things. Woojin hated that phrase. But right now there was nothing he could do except follow it. Should he call Yoona? It was 5:20 in the morning. She would be asleep beside her daughter.

He pressed print. Sixteen copies. A report with no page 7. No, there was a page 7. What used to be page 8 had simply become page 7. The report looked as if nothing was missing at all.

Seven o'clock sharp. President Moon Hyekyung walked in.

This time she sat down. That was different from last week. And she did not tell anyone to close the report. She turned the first page instead.

Kwon Jaehyuk stood up.

"Last week I said we would cut it to three clicks. I was wrong."

The room went quiet. CEO Hwang Inseok looked up.

"What we can do in two weeks is five. The last two steps need changes to the membership system and the payment module, and that takes six months. I've put the six-month plan on page 3."

Moon opened page 3. She looked at it for a long time. She picked up her red pen but didn't draw a single line.

"It's been a while since someone told me they were wrong."

Nobody could tell whether that was praise. Moon turned to the next page. Then she stopped at page 7.

"The Yeonhwi coupon."

VP Eom Gitae's shoulders stiffened. By the door, Woojin swallowed.

"I heard about this. A complaint from the department store."

"Yes, ma'am. I've already made it very clear to VP Kwon..."

"Seven out of ten were first-time buyers in those twenty-two hours?"

Moon wasn't listening to Eom. Her eyes were on the numbers.

"VP Eom. I asked you last week for the average age of a Yeonhwi customer. Did you find out?"

"...Fifty-eight."

"And the people who bought in those twenty-two hours?"

Eom couldn't answer. Kwon answered for him.

"Thirty-four."

A murmur went around the room. A gap of twenty-four years. Customers Yeonhwi had not met in thirty years had found it in twenty-two hours.

"VP Eom. I'm not telling you to discount Yeonhwi."

Moon said it.

"Bring me a way for a thirty-four-year-old to buy Yeonhwi without a discount. By next month. Online only."

Eom's face turned red. He lowered his head. And with his head still lowered, he glanced toward Kwon for just a moment. Woojin saw that look. It was far colder than the one he'd seen at the elevator last week.

Next up was the Overseas Biz. Team. The first report on the question Moon had thrown at them last week: why isn't Southeast Asia working?

VP Jin Manseok, Head of the Overseas Biz. Team, began turning pages. The same man who had "just happened to drop by" the sundae-guk place twelve years ago. He had never once left the Overseas Biz. Team since then, and he had headed it for the past six years. Page 1, market overview. Page 2, economic indicators by country. Page 3, local distribution structure. Only on page 4 did a sales graph appear, and from page 5 came the analysis of causes.

'Weak sales capability of local distribution partners.' 'Loss of price competitiveness due to exchange rates.' 'Aggressive low-price policies by competitors.' 'Insufficient marketing support from headquarters.'

Moon put down her pen.

"VP Jin, what page is the conclusion on?"

"Page 11, ma'am."

"Then start from page 11."

Jin flipped the pages in a hurry. Page 11 read: 'Review of distribution partner replacement and request for increased marketing budget.'

"Page 5 lists four causes. Distribution partner, exchange rate, competitors, headquarters. Where in there is something your department did?"

The room froze.

"Blame the partner, blame the exchange rate, blame the competitors, blame headquarters. That isn't a report. It's commentary."

Moon closed the report.

"Do it again next week. Conclusion on the first page. What we did wrong. What we'll change. How much better it gets if we change it. By when. And if the company needs to help, put that last. Like the online team's report."

Kwon's expression didn't change at all. But Woojin saw it. The look VP Jin gave Kwon on his way out of the room. Praise sometimes made one more enemy. In this company, "like the online team" was the last thing any other team wanted to hear.

The meeting ended at 7:48. Four minutes earlier than last week.

On his way out, CEO Hwang stopped Eom in front of the elevator. Woojin heard them while collecting the printouts.

"VP Eom, please do a good job on next month's plan. The President asked for it herself."

"Yes, sir."

"And don't fight VP Kwon too hard. Right now, his numbers are good."

*Right now.* Woojin didn't write that in the minutes. He couldn't. But in the minutes inside his head, it stayed in bold. This was exactly what Han Jiseok had meant by the second set of minutes.

8:10. Everyone had left the main conference room except Kwon, who was sorting his papers alone. The door opened again and Choi Yoona came in. During the meeting she had been sitting in the extra chairs by the door. Turning the printout pages, she must have seen that page 7 had changed. She had said nothing the whole meeting.

"Sir."

Kwon looked up. There were only the two of them in the room. Woojin, coming back for the leftover printouts, stopped in front of the half-open door.

"Why did you take my page out?"

"Because of the order of things."

"The order of things?"

"If that page had gone in today, the President would have asked today: 'Then why do we have an online team at all?' We don't have the numbers to answer that yet. The results of cutting it to five clicks come out next week. We bring those results first, and then we show her your page, Manager Choi."

"If you take the numbers out, you're deceiving the President."

"It isn't deceiving. It's deciding the order."

"And who decides the order?"

Kwon didn't answer. For the first time, Yoona's voice shook.

"Sir. I got up at 4:30 this morning. My daughter woke up and her mother wasn't there. I packed her lunch last night and left. I stayed up three nights this week to make that page. So what did I come in for today?"

Not a sound in the big room. Outside the door, Woojin held his breath too. This time, nobody could cover for anyone.

Kwon stood up.

"I'm sorry, Manager Choi."

He bowed his head to her. Woojin saw it through the gap in the door. He had never seen a VP bow his head to a manager before.

"Next month, that page goes up under your name. I promise."

Yoona stood there for a long time, then left the room without a word. She passed Woojin at the door and didn't look up.

That afternoon, outside the break room, Woojin saw that Yoona's bag was half open. Inside was a folded sheet of paper. A graph with a gray area shaded in. She had printed one copy for herself.

That one sheet would land on the conference table again, but not next month.

It would be much later than anyone thought.

(End of Episode 8)
