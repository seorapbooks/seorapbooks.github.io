# Episode 7. Yeonhwi Does Not Discount

It took forty seconds to take the coupon down.

Lee Woojin clicked *Deactivate* on the admin screen and refreshed the Yeonhwi page on the brand mall. The 10% discount label was gone. He let out one breath. Then he opened the coupon usage log.

From 4:12 p.m. yesterday to 2:18 p.m. today. Twenty-two hours. The number of times the coupon had been used on Yeonhwi products sat on the screen.

Woojin couldn't believe it. It was more than the number of Yeonhwi orders on the brand mall in the entire past month.

"Woojin."

Han Jiseok was standing behind him. Woojin couldn't turn around.

"It was me. I thought I'd set it to Slowash only, but the scope was on the default..."

"I know. Let's go to the VP's office. Now."

The door to the VP's office was open. VP Eom Gitae's voice carried all the way down the corridor. It wasn't a phone call. He had come up in person.

"VP Kwon, do you know what kind of brand Yeonhwi is? It has held the first floor of a department store for thirty years. Our customers have never seen Yeonhwi on sale. Not once."

"VP Eom, the coupon is down now."

"Down, and that's the end of it? This morning the store manager called me. A customer showed her phone at the counter and asked why it was full price in the store when it was 10% cheaper online. In thirty years, nobody has ever asked that question."

Kwon Jaehyuk was standing in front of his desk. Eom did not sit down. Both men were standing.

Woojin opened his mouth at the door. *It was me.* Those three words would have been enough. But another voice came first.

"I ordered it."

It was Baek Seungwon. He walked past Woojin and Han and into the office.

"I told them to test a first-purchase coupon. Not checking the scope properly was my mistake. I'm sorry, VP Eom."

Eom turned to look at him. Woojin stopped breathing. Baek had never given that order. Yesterday afternoon he had been in a division meeting.

"You, Director Baek?"

"Yes."

"Weren't you a department store buyer? You of all people should know what a discount means there."

"That's why I'm even more sorry."

Baek bowed deeply. Eom looked down at him for a long moment, then turned back to Kwon.

"VP Kwon. I'll say it again. Yeonhwi is not a brand that discounts."

"I know."

"And yet on Saturday, in front of the President, you said you'd let people buy in three clicks. I said it that day: easy means cheap. I said it would end like this."

Kwon said nothing for a moment. Then he picked up a printout from his desk and held it out to Eom.

"VP Eom, would you take a look at this?"

It was the coupon usage log Woojin had just printed. He had no idea when Kwon had taken it.

Eom didn't take the paper.

"What is that?"

"The people who bought Yeonhwi on the brand mall in those twenty-two hours. More than in the whole past month. And seven out of ten bought Yeonhwi for the first time. Customers who never came to the department store."

"They bought because it was discounted."

"It was 10%. If 10% is what makes someone buy for the first time, it may mean they wanted Yeonhwi and had no way to get it."

"How would you know that?"

"I don't. That's why I want to find out."

Eom's face hardened. In the end he turned away without taking the paper. At the door, his eyes met Woojin's. He looked at Woojin for only a second. It was a look that said he knew everything. Who had registered the coupon. What Baek had covered.

"VP Kwon. From now on, nothing involving Yeonhwi happens without my division's written consent. That includes the pop-ups."

He left. His footsteps echoed down the corridor for a long time.

Four of them were left in the office. Kwon sat down first.

"Senior Manager Han, Manager Lee. Get the pop-up agreements from every division except Yeonhwi. I'll handle Yeonhwi myself. Step out for a moment."

Han pulled Woojin out by the arm. Just before the door closed, Woojin saw Kwon loosen his tie.

Once the door shut, Kwon's voice dropped. Woojin had never heard that voice before - the one leaking through the gap in the door.

"Seungwon. You were in the division meeting yesterday."

"Hyung, just let it go."

"Are you going to do this every time? Cover everyone's mistakes with your own name?"

"Woojin's name shouldn't go up to a room like that yet. You know that, hyung."

A long silence. Woojin held his breath at the door. Han tugged him by the shoulder. It meant: this isn't a conversation we should hear.

"...This is the last time."

"Yes, hyung."

The door opened and Baek came out. His face said nothing had happened.

The moment they were in the corridor, Woojin caught Baek's arm.

"Director, why did you do that? It was me."

Baek didn't stop walking. Only at the elevators did he turn to look at Woojin.

"Woojin. Your name shouldn't go up to a room like that yet."

"Still."

"My name's already up there plenty. One more won't even show."

He smiled. To Woojin, that smile hurt in a strange way.

"Just promise me one thing."

"Anything."

"Next time you register a coupon, check the scope field three times. Three."

The elevator doors opened. Baek got in alone. Before the doors closed, he had his phone out, calling someone.

"Honey, I'll be late again today. Sorry."

At 6 that evening, Baek was not at the office but at a department store.

Shinwoo Department Store, main branch, first floor. The Yeonhwi counter sat right in front of the escalator - the best spot on the floor. Glass cases glittered under gold lights. Baek knew that spot well. Twenty years ago, when he was a department store buyer, he had been at the negotiating table that put Yeonhwi there.

The counter manager, Pyeon Misuk, was a woman past fifty. Twenty-five years at Yeonhwi and nowhere else. She recognized him.

"Buyer Baek? No - it's Director now, isn't it."

"It's been a long time, Manager Pyeon. I came about this morning. I'm sorry."

He bowed. The manager waved her hands, flustered.

"Why are *you* apologizing, Director? The coupon was the company's doing."

"I'm in that company."

The manager looked at him for a moment, then brought two stools out from behind the display case. They sat side by side in a corner of the counter.

§IMG3§

"Director, may I be honest?"

"Please."

"That customer this morning wasn't angry. She looked a little over thirty. She showed me her phone and said, 'I'm buying a birthday present for my mom. If it's cheaper online, I'll buy it there.' Then she just left. She didn't even get upset."

Baek said nothing.

"Young customers these days come in, look at the product, and check the price on their phone first. In twenty-five years we never had customers like that. That's why everyone's angry. Not at the customers. At a world we don't understand."

Baek turned that over for a long time. Before leaving, he bought one jar of Yeonhwi cream at full price. A present for his wife, Jung Miran. He didn't take a discount.

7 p.m. Woojin sat at his desk and opened the coupon log again. Twenty-two hours. Seven out of ten first-time buyers. He saved the numbers in a separate file. He started to name it *Yeonhwi_coupon_incident_analysis*, then changed it to *Yeonhwi_new_customers_22h*. The same numbers. Only the name was different.

Choi Yoona, at the next desk, glanced at his screen while packing up. Data and CRM. The person in the company who read numbers most accurately.

"Manager Lee, can I have that?"

"This? It's from the incident."

"Numbers from an incident are the most honest kind."

Yoona took the file. At 11 that night, she left a short message in the team chat.

[I made one page for this Saturday's report. It's not about Yeonhwi. It's about our company.]

The next morning, Woojin opened that page and couldn't say anything for a while.

(End of Episode 7)
