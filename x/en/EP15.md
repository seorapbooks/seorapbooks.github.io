# Episode 15. The Woman Who Trusts Excel - Choi Yoona's Story

Summer 1995, Ansan, Gyeonggi Province. A stationery shop, 「Yoona Stationery」.

Naming the shop after his daughter had been her father Choi Byeongcheol's stubborn idea. Eleven-year-old Choi Yoona was embarrassed by the sign. The kids in her class called her "Stationery."

The shop stood right in front of the school gate. At 8 a.m. the children poured in to buy their school supplies. Notebooks, colored paper, indoor shoes. Yoona helped her father from behind the counter. Her father never kept a ledger.

"Business is a gut feeling. Look at the kids' faces and you can see what'll sell next week."

His gut was often right. Just before Tamagotchis took off, he stocked ten of them and sold out in three days. The neighbors said Mr. Choi had the eyes of a ghost.

In the fall of 1997, his gut spoke again.

"This is going to blow up this winter."

He brought in three hundred character pencil cases imported from Japan. He borrowed to do it. That winter, the country fell apart. Every day the news said another factory had shut its doors. The children came to school with last year's pencil cases instead of new ones.

Of the three hundred, forty-one sold.

Yoona remembered that number exactly. On the night they stacked the leftover boxes in the storeroom, she took out a notebook and kept a ledger for the first time. How many came in, how many sold, how many were left. The numbers her father had never written down.

§IMG2§

Numbers were slower than gut feeling. But numbers didn't lie.

The next spring, the 「Yoona Stationery」 sign came down. On the day he took it down, her father said to her,

"I'm sorry, Yoona. Dad trusted his gut too much."

Yoona didn't answer. But she never threw that notebook away.

2008, Euljiro, Seoul. The customer analytics team at a credit card company.

Yoona had majored in statistics and joined a card company. Her first job was churn analysis. Finding the patterns in the people who canceled their cards every month.

Her team leader, Ha Junseok, was a man with good instincts.

"Women in their twenties won't leave if we just raise the points. I've done this for fifteen years. I know."

Yoona ran the data. Seventy percent of the women in their twenties who left were leaving because of the annual fee, not the points. She put the result in a report. Ha sent the report back.

"Ms. Choi, data is the past. We're the people who look at the future."

The point rewards went up. The churn rate stayed where it was. Six months later, customers in their twenties left in droves for a rival that waived the annual fee. The blame was written up as the analytics team's "forecasting failure." Ha was promoted the following year.

That night Yoona cried in the restroom. Not because she was sad. Because the storeroom from 1997 came back to her.

2012. She joined Cheongyeon Cosmetics as an experienced hire, in charge of customer relationship management.

At the interview, the executive in charge of online at the time asked her,

"Ms. Choi, would you say you have good instincts, or good data?"

"I tend to doubt my instincts until the numbers confirm them."

The executive laughed. And he hired her. Two years later he left the company. After that, no one ever hired her with a laugh like that again. In every meeting she heard the same thing. Manager Choi only looks at numbers. People's hearts don't show up in Excel.

She didn't argue back. Instead, she brought numbers no one could argue with.

April 2021, Monday, 10 p.m. The 29th floor of Cheongyeon Tower.

Yoona was the only one left in the office. On her monitor was the conversion graph for the Sugyeol cream product page. 1.7 times. The number the whole part had celebrated.

She didn't trust it.

The same week, the brand mall had run a store-wide coupon event. Fifteen percent off. The effect of the new page and the effect of the coupon were mixed together. She split the data in two. Customers who used the coupon and customers who didn't.

Midnight passed. Her phone rang. Her husband, Choi Jeonghun, just off work at a site in Sejong.

"Still at the office?"

§IMG3§

"Yeah. Just checking one number."

"Mom put Seoa to bed. Did you eat dinner?"

"I did."

She hadn't. Her husband knew. He didn't ask again. They had met at a statistics society in college. He was the only person who knew what her face looked like in front of a number.

"Yoona."

"Yeah."

"You're thinking about that notebook again, aren't you."

Yoona smiled. Before they married, she had once shown him the 「Yoona Stationery」 ledger. The notebook was still on the top shelf of the bookcase at home.

1:20 a.m. The result came out.

Even looking only at customers who hadn't used the coupon, the new page converted 1.4 times better. The coupon had inflated it by 0.3, but the page effect was real.

Yoona leaned back in her chair. For the first time, she thought she could trust the number. She put together a one-page report and uploaded it to the team's shared folder. The title was short.

'Sugyeol page effect - 1.4x excluding coupons. It's real.'

Tuesday, 8:10 a.m.

Han Jiseok opened the file. He looked at the screen for a long time, then walked over to Yoona's desk.

"Manager Choi. When did you do this?"

"Last night."

"Everyone was happy with 1.7. And you went and made it smaller."

Yoona didn't take her eyes off the monitor.

"The 1.7 will break next month. When the coupons end. Then when the President asks why it dropped, we won't have an answer. The 1.4 won't break."

Jiseok was quiet for a moment. Then he nodded.

"We'll go with 1.4 on Saturday."

At the next desk, Woojin had been listening. That day, for the first time, he thought he understood a little why Yoona added "not certain" to everything she said in meetings.

Saturday, 7:35 a.m. The boardroom.

Yoona reported the number. 1.4 times. She added that it was the figure with the coupon effect taken out.

President Moon picked up her red ballpoint pen.

"I heard 1.7. From Corporate Planning."

A few people in the room glanced toward the Corporate Planning seats. Senior Manager Sung Dongmin shuffled his papers. Yoona answered.

"The 1.7 includes the coupon event. It will go down when the coupons end. The number we can stand behind is 1.4."

President Moon looked at her for a long time.

§IMG4§

"It's rare for someone to walk in with a smaller number."

Then she drew a single circle on the printout.

After the meeting, Yoona took out her phone. She sent a message to her father in Ansan. Seventy years old. These days he worked in the guard booth of a local apartment complex.

[Dad, I got praised at work today.]

The answer came quickly. Her father still typed slowly, but he was always fast with his daughter's messages.

[Good job. For what?]

Yoona thought for a moment, then typed her reply.

[For making a number smaller.]

(End of Episode 15)
