# Episode 12. The Division Strikes Back

Saturday, 7 p.m. The ring finally came out of the drawer.

Yoon Chaewon looked down at the navy box for a long time. The candle at 「Summer Night」, a bistro in Seochon, flickered over the ring. Woojin got through about half of what he had prepared. He couldn't remember the other half. A man who got stuck on a sentence he had rewritten thirteen times was never going to finish one he hadn't practiced even once.

Chaewon didn't take the ring out. Instead, she slowly closed the box.

"Woojin. Let me ask you one thing."

"Okay."

"If we get married, will you be home on Saturday mornings?"

Woojin couldn't answer. He could have lied. But she was someone who could read his mood from a single period at the end of a text.

"...Not for a while."

Chaewon smiled. Not sad, not angry. A very tired smile.

"I'll think about it. Can I keep this?"

The ring had left the drawer, but it wasn't on her finger yet.

Monday, 9 a.m. Director Cho Youngseok had settled himself on the chair next to Woojin's desk.

"Manager Lee, that body wash subscription proposal. Let me have the file. It's our division's product, isn't it? I'll take a look and give you some feedback."

A bulk body wash subscription. The Online Planning Part had spent two weeks on it. An online-only product that bundled Slowash body wash in large sizes for regular delivery. Woojin had pulled the customer data, Na Seohyun had researched the subscription models at SODA LAB and MINT&, and Han Jiseok had rebuilt the cost structure. It was set to go up at next Saturday's meeting.

"Sir, Head of Part Han is still reviewing it."

"Come on, we can review it together. It's our product."

Cho smiled and patted Woojin's shoulder. The hand was heavy. Fifty-two years old. Director in charge of e-commerce for the Daily Beauty Biz. Division. An apartment in Gangnam, a son, Cho Hyunwoo, in his last year of high school, and one last shot at a promotion to executive, next year.

Woojin messaged Han Jiseok. The reply was short.

[Give it to him. We need that division's cooperation anyway.]

Woojin sent the file. It took him three days to realize it was a mistake.

Thursday afternoon. The division heads' meeting, chaired by the CEO.

Kwon Jaehyuk went in and came out with a stiff face. He called Han Jiseok and Baek Seungwon to his office right away. Woojin stood at the door.

"The Daily Beauty Biz. Division reported a new business at today's CEO meeting."

Kwon put a single printout on the desk.

'Daily Beauty Biz. Division - Plan for an Online-Only Bulk Subscription Product.'

Han Jiseok didn't need to turn past the first page. The table structure, the colors of the graphs, even where the commas fell in the sentences. It was the proposal he had polished for two weeks. Only the cover was different.

"The CEO loved it. He said the divisions are finally starting to understand online."

Han Jiseok's face went white. Woojin had never seen that face before. That day he learned that when a calm man gets angry, the color leaves his face first.

"I'll go and see Director Cho."

"Jiseok."

Baek Seungwon grabbed his arm.

"Let go, sir. This isn't right. Woojin and Seohyun stayed up for two weeks making this."

"I know. That's why you can't go."

"Why not?"

"If you go, it turns into a fight. If it's a fight, what the CEO sees isn't the proposal. It's the online team fighting with a division. Then we lose. The CEO is on the division's side. The division is inside people, and our VP came from outside."

Kwon said nothing. That silence meant Baek was right.

"Then what do we do? Just give it to them?"

"No."

Baek loosened his tie.

"This isn't something you fight with numbers. I'll handle it."

After they left the VP's office, Woojin and Na Seohyun ran into each other in the pantry. The news had already spread through the whole part.

"Sir, is it true? They just changed the cover on our proposal?"

"Yeah."

Seohyun crushed her paper cup.

"Isn't this a straight-up reason to quit?"

Woojin couldn't laugh. Twenty-six. Six months at the company. For her generation, something like this wasn't something you put up with. It was a reason to leave.

"So if something like this happens, you'd quit right away?"

"Hmm... Not this time."

"Why not?"

"Because I want to see what Director Baek does."

She tossed the crushed cup at the trash can. It went in on the first try.

"My parents' generation put up with everything the company told them to. I won't. But even not putting up with it, I want to decide after I see something."

Woojin would remember that for a long time. Between the generation that endures and the generation that doesn't, he still didn't know which side he was on.

6 p.m. The emergency stairwell on the 29th floor.

Han Jiseok was leaning on the railing. Baek Seungwon opened the door and came in. Just the two of them.

"Hyung."

At work, Han called Baek hyung, big brother, only in this stairwell. It had been that way since the first night they stayed up together on the same team ten years ago. Once they walked back through the office door, he was "Director" again.

§IMG2§

"Hyung, this really isn't right. The kids stayed up for two weeks."

"I know, Jiseok."

"You always cover things up, so people think they can just take our team's work."

Baek didn't answer. Instead, he took two sticks of gum from his pocket and handed one to Han. Three years since they had quit smoking. Both of them were getting by on gum.

"Jiseok. I'll meet Director Cho tonight."

"And do what? Drink again and give it all away?"

"Yeah. I'll give something away. But not all of it."

"What are you keeping?"

"The kids' names."

Han stopped chewing. Baek patted his shoulder twice.

"Trust me. You buy the kids dinner tonight. I hear Seohyun's talking about quitting."

Han burst out laughing. Laughing while still angry was a strange feeling.

"How do you know that?"

"I know everything. That's my job."

The stairwell door opened and closed. Baek walked back to the office as Director Baek again.

9 p.m. that night. A pojangmacha behind the office, 「Alley's End Pocha」.

Four tables under a plastic tent. A good spot for two. Baek Seungwon and Cho Youngseok. The first bottle of soju was gone fast. Fish cake skewers simmered in a broth pot by the entrance, and the heater under the table smelled faintly of kerosene. Cho ordered a plate of stir-fried spicy octopus and didn't touch it.

§IMG3§

"Director Baek, you're not here to have it out with me, are you?"

"Would it change anything if I did?"

Cho laughed. Then he opened a second bottle.

"I'll be honest. I'm sorry. But next year is my last chance. You know that. If I don't make executive by fifty-three, there's nothing after that."

"I know."

"My son's a senior in high school. If he has to retake the exam, that's another year. Fifteen years left on the mortgage. The apartment's gone up in value, but you can't do anything with that. If I sell, where do I go?"

Cho kept talking. The Gangnam apartment, the cram school fees, his wife Park Sunyoung's sighs. The things nobody had asked about at the window table on the night of the split dinner. Back then they had sounded like bragging. Not now.

"I had to show something from our division too. Online-only, subscriptions, that kind of thing. But my people can't build that. For twenty years all they've done is push product into supermarkets."

The second bottle was empty. Baek ordered a third.

"Sir."

"Yeah."

"Let's make that proposal a joint one."

Cho's glass stopped halfway.

"The Daily Beauty Biz. Division takes the product, and the online team takes the sales structure. We take it up together at the President's meeting next Saturday. Your name first, our team's name after."

"...You'd do that?"

"Just one thing. Put the names of the people who did the work on the last page of the deck. Lee Woojin, Na Seohyun. They stayed up nights making it."

Cho looked at Baek for a long time. Then he poured the first glass from the third bottle and set it in front of him.

"Director Baek. Why do you live like this?"

"Like what?"

"Always putting other people's names first. That's why you're still a director."

Baek smiled. He didn't answer.

11:30 p.m. Three bottles empty. Cho called a designated driver and left first. Baek stood alone in front of the pojangmacha and breathed in the night air. It was April, but the night wind was cold.

He took out his phone. Two missed calls. His wife, Jung Miran.

"Honey, I'm sorry. I'm late, aren't I."

"Drinking on someone else's behalf again?"

"Yeah."

On the other end, his wife laughed. The laugh of someone who had worked as a pharmacist for twenty years. A laugh that knew what her husband needed on a night like this wasn't a lecture.

"Come home. I'll make you some honey water."

"I'm sorry."

"You'll be sorry tomorrow too."

"Yeah. Probably."

Baek hung up and walked. He didn't take a taxi. It was forty minutes home. He decided not to think about anything for those forty minutes. Then he thought about just one thing. The two names he had kept today.

That December, Cheongyeon Group announced its list of newly promoted executives.

Baek Seungwon's name was not on it.
