# Episode 2. The 29th Floor at 5:30 A.M.

§IMG1§

2:14 a.m. Cheongyeon Tower, 29th floor.

There were eleven questions on the whiteboard. They'd been told to write ten, but Han Jiseok had added one more. Nobody suggested erasing it.

*Why have we ended up like this?*

That sentence at the top had sat there for hours without an answer.

"Back from the convenience store."

Director Baek Seungwon came in with two plastic bags. Four cup noodles, eight triangle kimbap, four hangover drinks, and one chocolate milk.

"Who's the chocolate milk for?"

"Woojin. You're still a baby."

"I'm thirty-six."

"Like I said. A baby."

Baek laughed and peeled back the lids one by one. As he poured the water, he told them about the dinner. How Director Cho had ended up passing around apartment price charts on his phone. How the juniors had tried to go on to karaoke, got caught by the five-person rule, split into two rooms, and sang together by pounding on the shared wall.

Listening, Han laughed for the first time that night. Woojin understood. Baek hadn't gone out for noodles. He'd left for thirty minutes to change the air that had gone stiff in front of the VP.

VP Kwon Jaehyuk sat alone in a corner of the conference room, looking at his laptop. He hadn't touched his noodles.

"Sir, please eat while you work."

"You all go ahead."

He didn't take his eyes off the screen. On it were the numbers Han had pulled in an hour and a half. A year of sales from the Cheongyeon brand mall. The year everyone had rushed online because of COVID. The numbers had gone up. The problem was that everyone else's had gone up much more.

*The enemy outside.*

Han put that table on page two of the report. Online growth rates for Cheongyeon, SODA LAB, and the two new indie brands close on SODA LAB's heels, MINT& and DAYBREEZE. When he drew the bar chart, Cheongyeon's bar was the shortest. Standing next to everyone else's, it looked even shorter.

3 a.m. The office phone rang. A landline ringing at this hour was never good news. Woojin picked up.

§IMG2§

"This is the 29th floor? Tak Mingyu, night shift, building security. The lights on your floor have been on all night. Is something wrong?"

"No, we're working."

"...At this hour?"

"Yes."

"On a Friday night?"

"Yes."

A brief silence on the line.

"Then I'll log it as 'urgent work.' What should I put as the reason?"

Woojin looked at the whiteboard. *Why have we ended up like this?*

"...A report. Just put 'report.'"

When he hung up, Baek snickered.

"Security's saner than we are."

3:30 a.m. Han revised the conclusion for the first time.

*The slowdown in online growth is a structural phenomenon driven by intensified competition and market saturation.*

Kwon read it and said,

"That's the sentence the President hates most."

"Why?"

"Because it says it's not our fault."

Han deleted it. He wrote it again.

*The core cause of the slowdown in online growth is our lack of execution speed and customer experience.*

This time Baek shook his head.

"Jiseok, that's too harsh. Write that, and our team is the culprit from the very first meeting."

"Then how do I write it?"

"A sentence with no culprit but with a solution."

"There's no such sentence."

"That's why reports are hard."

4:50 a.m. The third conclusion.

*To restore online growth, we will make improving the customer experience our top priority and report an action plan within four weeks.*

Kwon studied it for a long time.

"Four weeks is too long. Make it two."

"We don't know yet what we can do in two weeks."

"You can find that out next week. What the President wants to hear tomorrow is *when*."

Han erased the "4" and wrote "2." Changing one number took one second. He knew exactly how that one second would change the next two weeks.

Next, Kwon flipped the order of the report. In the thirty-page deck, the conclusion had been on page twenty-nine. You only got there after market overview, competitor analysis, internal status, and root-cause analysis.

"Why is the conclusion at the very end?"

"We built it up in logical order..."

"The President closes it long before page twenty-nine. Conclusion on page one. Diagnosis on page two. Options on page three. Page four: what improves, and by how much, if we do it. Page five: who does what by when. Page six: what we need from the company. Everything else goes in the appendix."

"We put in requests too?"

"We have to. There are things we can't do on our own. Pop-ups that need the business divisions. Security reviews. If we don't say it in front of the President, next week it becomes the reason we failed. And then it sounds like blaming others."

Han reordered the pages. Six of the thirty moved to the front. The other twenty-four went behind a divider labeled *Appendix*. Most of the analysis they'd built all night became appendix in that moment. Woojin felt a little cheated. Han looked unbothered. Three years of lab notebooks at the research center, he said later, had ended up as a single line on a product detail page.

Only then did Kwon pick up his cup noodles. The water had been poured three hours earlier. The noodles had soaked up all the broth and turned into a lump. He ate it as if nothing were wrong.

"Sir, shall I make you a fresh one?"

Woojin asked. Kwon shook his head.

"Cold noodles taste best. When I first went to Hong Kong, I ate these every night for three years. I was the only Korean in the office."

Woojin had never heard this. Kwon was known only as the executive brilliantly recruited from a global company.

"I learned one thing back then. Good products don't sell because they're good. They sell because there's a structure that sells them."

He lifted the last lump of noodles with his chopsticks.

"Our company has good products. What it doesn't have is a structure."

With that, he went back to his laptop. Clearing away the empty cups, Woojin caught Baek's eye. Baek gave a small nod. It seemed to mean: *He's better than you'd think.*

In the meantime, Woojin fell asleep on the conference-room sofa for about forty minutes. When he woke, Baek's coat was draped over him. Baek, in his shirtsleeves, was putting the printouts in order.

5 a.m. Rehearsal began.

"I'll be the President."

Baek undid his tie and started to wrap it around his head, then thought better of it and took it off again. He stood at the end of the table and lowered his voice.

"VP Kwon. You say online sales went up. Then why did market share fall?"

"Because the market grew faster. While we grew twenty percent, the market..."

"That's a reason. So what are you going to do about it?"

Kwon stopped. Baek looked a little startled. He'd meant it as a joke, and the VP had genuinely stalled.

"Again. Director Baek, ask me again."

A second time, a third. Kwon answered the same question three different ways. The third answer was one sentence.

"Within two weeks, we will remove three pain points customers face on our mall, and report the results to you in numbers."

Baek dropped the impression and clapped.

"That's it. That's the one."

Han wrote the sentence down. It was the same as the conclusion. In the end, the report needed thirty pages to say that one sentence. And those thirty pages, most likely, nobody would ever turn. At the time, he thought that was a joke.

5:30 a.m.

Woojin switched on the lights in the main conference room on the 29th floor. An oval table that seated sixteen. Outside it was still dark, and the glass reflected only the lit room and his own face.

His phone rang. Manager Yang Eunju, secretary in the holding company's President's office. She had served President Moon Hyekyung for ten years. She was always the first to know the President's moods and schedule.

"Manager Lee, you're in early. Let me walk you through the setup for President Moon's meeting. Write this down."

Woojin took out his notebook.

"Water at room temperature. No chilled bottles. No straws. Glasses, not paper cups. Printed materials, 14-point font or larger, one staple at an angle in the top left. Put the laser pointer away. The President doesn't like people pointing at the screen."

"Yes."

"And in front of the President's seat, two pens. One black, one red."

"Red?"

"She draws red lines on reports. A lot of them."

Woojin stopped writing.

"Manager Yang, what does the President dislike most?"

A short laugh on the other end.

"The answer 'I don't know.' Give a wrong number instead. Say you don't know, and the meeting ends right there."

After hanging up, Woojin brought sixteen bottles of water from the room-temperature storeroom. He polished sixteen glasses. He stapled sixteen printouts at an angle in the top left. Three whose angle was off, he reprinted.

§IMG3§

He set two pens in front of the President's seat. Black, and red.

6:20. Han came into the conference room and picked up a printout. He read the conclusion again. He took out a pen to fix something, then stopped.

"Sir, you can't change it now. All sixteen copies are printed."

"I know."

Han put the pen in his pocket. Then he went to the window and looked toward the sunrise. The sky across the river was slowly growing lighter.

"Woojin."

"Yes?"

"My son's nine. When he wakes up this morning, his dad won't be there."

"..."

"It's his first swimming lesson today. Nine o'clock. He's been saying all week he's scared to get in the water."

Instead of answering, Woojin polished one more glass. It was already clean.

6:40. People began to arrive.

VP Eom Gitae, Head of the luxury herbal brand Yeonhwi, came first. He picked up a printout, looked only at the cover, and put it down. People from Corporate Planning, Finance, and the IT Platform team filled the seats one after another. They all wore ties, they all looked tired, and they all wore the face of someone waiting for something.

6:50. Hwang Inseok walked in. CEO of Cheongyeon Cosmetics. His business card said CEO, but within the group his rank was SVP. Between him and Moon Hyekyung, President of the holding company, stood one rung: EVP. That distance set the air in the room that morning. A dark navy suit, as befitted someone from a global consulting firm. He shook Kwon's hand briefly and whispered in his ear.

"I hear the President bought something on the SODA LAB mall herself last night."

Kwon's expression didn't change. But he turned again to page two of the printout.

6:58. The room went quiet.

Baek straightened his tie once more. Han gripped his pen, then let it go. Woojin stood by the door and steadied his breathing.

7:00 sharp.

The door opened, and President Moon Hyekyung walked in.

§IMG4§

Gray jacket, low-heeled shoes, a single phone in her hand. She didn't sit. She didn't look at the sixteen printouts, the room-temperature water, the glasses, or the black and red pens.

She stood at the end of the table and looked around the room once. Then she spoke her first sentence.

"Close the reports."

Sixteen printouts closed at once.

"Everyone, take out your phones."

(End of Episode 2)
