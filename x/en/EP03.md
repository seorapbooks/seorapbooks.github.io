# Episode 3. Nine Clicks to Checkout

§IMG1§

"Everyone, take out your phones."

Sixteen people reached into their pockets at once. Some from inside their jackets, some from briefcases. VP Eom Gitae searched for a long time before realizing he'd left his in the secretary's office. Woojin quietly handed him his own.

President Moon Hyekyung was still standing.

"Open a browser and go to two sites. One is our brand mall. The other is..."

"...SODA LAB."

The name she said was SODA LAB. The company whose overseas sales, five years after its founding, had caught up with Cheongyeon's Sugyeol brand. A few people in the room drew a very small breath.

"Buy the same thing on both. One moisturizing cream. Stop right before you press 'Pay.' Count how many times you click."

Only the sound of tapping fingers remained in the room.

Standing by the door, Woojin glanced at Han Jiseok's screen. The SODA LAB mall. The cream appeared on the first screen. Tap - one. "Buy Now" - two. The quick-pay screen - three. That was it. No sign-up, no login.

"Three."

Across the table, VP Oh Jeongtaek, Head of the Finance Team, said it first. He was always fastest when it came to numbers. Then from around the table: "Three." "Three." "Me too, three."

Moon nodded.

"Now our mall."

This time no one said a number first.

The Cheongyeon brand mall. Instead of cream, the first screen showed a pop-up: *Spring Member Appreciation Event*. Close it - one. Look for the cream under the "Skincare" menu - two. Into the Sugyeol brand shop - three. Tap the product - four. Tap "Purchase," and a login window appeared. Five.

Not a member, so tap "Sign Up." Six. There were seven terms and conditions. No "Agree to All" button. Han tapped seven times. He decided not to count those. Identity verification - seven.

"Huh?"

It was SVP & CEO Hwang Inseok. He frowned at his phone.

"The verification text isn't coming."

No one answered. The CEO was stuck at identity verification on his own company's online store. VP Eom, holding Woojin's phone, was stuck a little earlier. He was opening the terms one by one and reading them.

The verification text arrived on Han's screen. When he finished verifying, the screen went back to the beginning. His cart was empty.

"..."

Han found the cream again. Cart - eight. On the checkout screen, a window popped up: *Would you like to apply a coupon?* He had no coupons. "No" - nine.

Only then could he see the Pay button.

§IMG2§

"Nine."

Han said. His voice rang louder than he expected.

"Nine for me too."

"Mine's... eleven. The event pop-up came up twice."

"I'm still verifying."

The last was CEO Hwang. Someone in the room coughed to hold back a laugh.

Moon didn't laugh. For the first time, she pulled out a chair and sat. She didn't touch the room-temperature water.

"Three versus nine. A customer has to tap six more times to buy our cream. How many leave somewhere in those six?"

No one answered.

"VP Eom."

Eom looked up, still holding Woojin's phone.

"How old is your daughter?"

"Twenty-nine."

"Where does she buy her cosmetics?"

"That's... I'm not sure."

"Not 'I'm not sure.'"

By the door, Woojin held his breath. He remembered Manager Yang's voice at 5:30 that morning. *Say you don't know, and the meeting ends right there.*

Eom was silent a moment, then said,

"Online... I think."

"Is that 'online' our mall?"

The room went quiet. Yeonhwi was the pride of the group. The first floor of every department store, the best spot in every duty-free shop. And the head of that brand's division didn't know where his own daughter bought cosmetics. And he'd been caught not knowing, in front of sixteen people.

"Yeonhwi customers buy at department stores. If we discount Yeonhwi online, the brand..."

"VP Eom. What's the average age of a Yeonhwi customer right now?"

"..."

"Their daughters don't go to department stores. And those daughters buy their mothers' cosmetics for them too. On their phones. In three taps."

Eom said nothing more.

Moon turned her head. Her gaze moved slowly along the table and stopped on one person.

"VP Kwon."

"Yes."

"Why does a customer have to tap nine times to buy one cream on our mall?"

Kwon Jaehyuk didn't answer.

One second.

Two seconds.

Three seconds.

Han started to speak. Why the brand mall overhaul had been delayed, how the IT budget had been cut twice, how every division demanded different pop-ups. He could explain all of it in numbers.

Under the table, Kwon's left hand moved very slightly. At an angle only Han could see. *Don't.*

"By next Saturday, I'll bring a way to cut it to three."

Moon's eyebrows rose, just slightly.

"Three?"

"Yes."

"What does IT say?"

"I haven't asked them yet."

"You're promising without asking?"

"If I ask, they'll say six months."

This time real laughter broke out in a few places around the room. Only the people from the IT Platform Group didn't laugh.

Moon looked at Kwon for a long time. Then she stood.

"All right. Next Saturday, 7 a.m."

7:52. Walking toward the door, she stopped at the end of the table. She picked up one of the closed printouts and opened it to page two. The bar chart of growth rates: Cheongyeon against SODA LAB, MINT&, and DAYBREEZE.

She picked up the red pen Woojin had placed there. She circled the shortest bar. Beside it, she wrote one word.

*Why?*

She put down the pen and left.

Sixteen untouched bottles of room-temperature water remained in the room.

"VP Kwon."

CEO Hwang patted Kwon on the shoulder. He had never finished verifying.

"Writing 'two weeks' was smart. But why promise three clicks?"

"Because it's easier than nine."

Hwang laughed. Whether the laugh was praise or warning, Woojin couldn't tell.

At the elevators, VP Eom stopped Kwon. Woojin stood two steps behind, holding the phone he'd gotten back.

"VP Kwon."

"Yes, sir."

"If you plan to turn Yeonhwi into something people buy in three taps, you'd better be ready."

"I'm not saying sell Yeonhwi cheap. I'm saying make it easy to buy."

"You think those are different? Online, easy means cheap."

The elevator doors opened. Eom stepped in first and pressed the close button. Just before the doors shut, he added one more thing.

§IMG3§

"People from outside always try to change the company in three years."

The doors closed. Kwon didn't wait for the next elevator. He walked toward the stairs. Watching his back, Woojin thought: this morning, he'd believed there was one enemy in that conference room. SODA LAB. But the enemy wasn't only outside.

8:10. The four of them gathered again in a small meeting room on the 29th floor.

Han spoke first.

"Sir, why did you stop me? I could have explained everything. The IT budget cuts, the divisions' pop-up requests, how the overhaul got delayed."

Kwon looked out the window.

"The President wasn't asking for reasons."

"Then what?"

"She was checking whether we're people who give reasons or people who bring solutions. In that room, a reason, however accurate, becomes an excuse."

Han didn't answer. He didn't look convinced. But he didn't look like he'd found a rebuttal either.

Kwon looked at his watch.

"Han, what time did you say your son's swimming lesson was?"

"...Nine."

"You can make it if you go now. Go. See you Monday."

Han stood blankly for a moment, then picked up his bag. On his way out, he bowed his head once. Kwon was already opening his laptop. Woojin would remember that scene for a long time. That the man who had stayed silent for three seconds remembered the time of someone else's child's swimming lesson.

9 a.m. Woojin was writing up the minutes when his fingers stopped on one line.

*Open item: cause of the nine-step checkout - answer insufficient.*

Should he keep this line? The fact that no one had answered the President's *Why?* He sat for a long time with the cursor blinking.

"Woojin, go home. You said you had plans today."

It was Director Baek.

"What about the minutes?"

"Do them Monday. Today's the day you act like a human being."

"What about you, sir?"

"I have a few things to talk about with the VP."

Baek smiled, but his eyes didn't. Woojin knew that face. It was the one he wore at team dinners just before covering for someone's mistake under his own name. What mistake from today's meeting needed covering, Woojin didn't know yet.

He closed his laptop and hurried to the elevator. Home, shower, change, then forty minutes to the brunch café, Mayday Table. By his math, it would be tight. Running to the subway, he messaged Chaewon.

[Leaving now. I won't be late today.]

This time the reply came right away.

[Liar. Photo proof.]

He snapped the sky as he ran and sent it. The Saturday-morning sky was almost too clear.

10:55 a.m. Mayday Table, a brunch café in Seochon.

For the first time in seven years, Woojin arrived before the agreed time. Chaewon came in five minutes later, saw him, and stopped.

§IMG4§

"What's this? You're not late today?"

"It's our seventh anniversary."

Chaewon laughed. Woojin laughed too. And, out of habit, he slipped his hand into his inside jacket pocket.

Empty.

The ring was on the 29th floor, in the bottom drawer of his desk.

"What? Did you lose something?"

"No. It's nothing."

His phone buzzed. A new group chat. Created by Baek Seungwon. Four members.

[Baek Seungwon] 5:30 again next week. Noodles are on me.
[Kwon Jaehyuk] Not two weeks. One. Everyone in by 7 on Monday.
[Han Jiseok] Yes, sir.

A moment later, the chat's name changed.

*Saturday, 7 A.M. (1/52)*

[Lee Woojin] What's the 52?
[Han Jiseok] There are 52 weeks in a year.
[Baek Seungwon] You think it'll only be a year?

Woojin put his phone face down. Across the table, Chaewon held out a menu.

"You said you'd be all mine today."

"I am. All day."

The ring in the drawer stayed where it was for a long time after that.

(End of Episode 3)
