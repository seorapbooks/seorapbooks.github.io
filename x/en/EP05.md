# Episode 5. The Soup House We Remember

§IMG1§

Autumn 2009. Han Jiseok was twenty-eight, in his second year at the research center.

The Color Cosmetics Marketing Team had borrowed one developer from the research center for three months ahead of a new product launch, and that developer was him. Two months in a tie instead of a white lab coat. He still tied it every morning by watching a video online.

"Jiseok, a guest is coming tonight. Come with me."

It was Director Seok Taeyong of the Color Cosmetics Marketing Team. Two years later, when Han moved to marketing for good, Seok would be his first boss. A loud voice, a strong head for alcohol, and a man who never forgot a junior's name.

"Who is it, sir?"

"Someone coming in from Hong Kong. Senior Manager at the Asia headquarters of a global beauty company. When we go into Hong Kong, we're going to borrow their distribution network. He's Korean, but he's been out there five years."

"Am I allowed to be there?"

"He wants to hear about the cream you made. He heard someone from the research center moved to marketing, and he's curious."

Doldam House, a *sundae-guk* place in the alley behind the office - a Korean pork soup house. Six tables, and a menu board on the wall coated in old grease. Seok had chosen it on purpose. Koreans who've lived abroad a long time, he said, are happiest in places like this.

Kwon Jaehyuk walked in at exactly 7. Gray suit, one briefcase. He said he was thirty-eight, but to Han he looked over forty. There was tiredness under his eyes.

"Kwon Jaehyuk. I'm not late, am I?"

"Right on time. This is Han Jiseok, a researcher. We borrowed him from the research center for a bit."

As he shook Han's hand, Kwon glanced down at it for a moment.

"Your hands aren't rough. You must have worn gloves in the lab."

"Yes. All three years."

"What formulas did you work on?"

§IMG2§

That night, Kwon listened to Han without noticing his soup going cold. The emulsion stability of a moisturizing cream. A texture that separated every summer. How changing the fragrance reduced skin irritation, but people disliked it because it smelled "cheap." Not even Seok had ever listened to that story all the way through.

"That cream - is it on the market now?"

"No. It was rejected at approval."

"Why?"

"The cost didn't work. And there was nowhere to sell it."

Kwon set down his soju glass.

"Jiseok. If a good formula never meets a structure that sells it, it's just a lab notebook."

Then he smiled. It wasn't a mocking smile. It was the smile of someone who had been through the same thing.

After 8, something strange happened.

The door of the soup house opened, and VP Jin Manseok, Head of the Overseas Biz. Team, walked in. "Senior Manager Kwon, I heard you were here." Fifteen minutes later came SVP Tak Sanghun, Head of the Domestic Sales Biz. Division. Then VP Ham Yeonggil, Head of the Duty-Free Biz. Division. Each of them said they were just passing by. A six-table soup house filled up with Cheongyeon executives.

Only then did Han understand. What this man held wasn't just one market called Hong Kong. For Cheongyeon, trying to get out into Asia, this tired-looking senior manager was a door.

And yet each time an executive arrived, Kwon stood, greeted them, and sat back down. Then he turned to Han.

"That fragrance story from earlier. Finish it."

The executives left one by one. By 10, only the original three were left at the table.

When they parted, Kwon handed Han his business card. On the back of the English card, he wrote something in ballpoint pen.

*Han Jiseok - emulsion stability. Lower irritation with fragrance change.*

"When I come next year, I'll ask what happened to that cream."

After they put Kwon in a taxi at the end of the alley, Seok lit a cigarette.

"So, Jiseok. How was tonight?"

"Strange. All those executives came one after another, and he kept asking only me."

Seok let out a long stream of smoke.

"There are two kinds of people who last at a company. People who remember the ones above them, and people who remember the ones below them."

"And Senior Manager Kwon?"

"The ones below. People like that don't rise fast. But when they do, people follow them to the end."

"Which kind are you, sir?"

Seok laughed and stubbed out his cigarette.

"I'm the kind who couldn't do either. So I'll end as a director."

It sounded like a joke. Six years later, Seok really did leave the company as a director. At his farewell party, Han remembered what he'd heard in that alley. And how few people came to the party.

The next spring, Kwon really did come back. The moment he saw Han in the hallway outside a meeting room, he said,

"Jiseok, what happened to that cream?"

A man he hadn't seen in a year remembered his name and his cream. That was the day Han decided to follow him. That one reason was enough.

March 2021. Monday, 7:30 p.m.

The soup house was still there. The sign had changed from *Doldam House* to *Doldam Sundae-guk*, there were eight tables now, and a notice on the wall said *No entry for groups of five or more*. Two people had no problem at all.

"It's the same in here."

Kwon said it looking at the menu board. The menu board alone was the same, right down to the grease.

"The owner changed, sir. The old owner retired three years ago."

"Everything changes. Only the people stay."

Kwon ordered two bowls of *sundae-guk* and a bottle of soju. The exact same order as twelve years ago.

§IMG3§

For a while they ate without a word. When the soup was half gone, Kwon spoke first.

"Did your son make it to swimming on Saturday?"

Han stopped with his spoon in midair. This was the man who had sent him off right after the meeting so he wouldn't be late for the 9 o'clock lesson.

"Yes, sir. He cried that he was scared to get in the water, and by the end he cried that he didn't want to get out."

Kwon laughed out loud. It was the first time Han had ever heard him laugh out loud.

"My daughter was the same. Now she's across the ocean and doesn't come home. She's in London."

"Doesn't she come during school breaks?"

"I can't go."

A short answer. Han didn't ask further. Then Kwon asked,

"That cream from back then - what happened in the end? Did it go into Sugyeol?"

Han put down his spoon.

"It didn't."

"Why not?"

"There was no structure to sell it."

Kwon smiled. The same smile as twelve years ago. Then he poured a glass of soju and set it in front of Han.

"Senior Manager Han. I'll be honest with you."

"Yes, sir."

"If I don't pull this off, I'm finished."

Han didn't pick up the glass.

"An executive brought in from outside gets a report card in two years. This is my second year. When President Moon says she'll run the Saturday meeting herself, it means she's going to read the report card herself."

"If you're finished, sir..."

"Then our team is finished too. Director Baek, you. That's why I'm telling you."

Kwon picked up his glass, then set it down again. Han had never seen him hesitate like this.

"I'm sorry. For taking all your Saturdays."

Han would remember those words for a long time. In twelve years, it was the first apology he had ever heard from this man. The words *and I'll take them tomorrow too* were missing from it, but they both knew.

"Sir."

"Yes."

"I still have the formula notebook for that cream."

Kwon looked up.

"This time, let's build the structure that sells it."

Instead of answering, Kwon touched his glass to Han's. A clear ring sounded through the old soup house.

9:40 p.m. As they left, Han's phone rang. It was Baek Seungwon.

"Jiseok, where are you?"

"Having dinner with the VP. What's going on?"

"Director Noh Seunghwan from the IT Platform Group called. He wants a meeting tomorrow at 9 a.m. About the brand mall improvements."

"That's good news."

"But he didn't sound good. He said, 'Come prepared.'"

Han looked back at Kwon. Kwon was gazing up at the soup house sign. A sign that hadn't been there twelve years ago.

"Sir, I know what he's going to say."

"What's he going to say?"

"Six months."

(End of Episode 5)
